## Facts verified
- **Harbour and history.**
  - Deep natural harbour, usable in all weathers (Sri Lanka Tourism, Wikipedia).
  - Known as Gokanna/Gokarna; the main port in the Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa eras (Rough Guides).
  - Held in turn by the Portuguese, Danish, Dutch, French and British. British from 1795 to 1948.
  - Japanese carrier attack on the harbour and airfield in April 1942.
- **Fort Frederick.**
  - Portuguese fort of 1623/1624, built from Koneswaram’s stones after the temple was destroyed
    (14 April 1622).
  - Dutch capture 1639, rebuilt 1665; British from 1795.
  - Garrisoned by the Gajaba Regiment and open to visitors. Wellesley Lodge is inside.
- **Koneswaram.**
  - On Swami Rock (Konesar Malai); one of the Pancha Ishwarams.
  - Rebuilt in the mid-20th century after underwater finds in the 1950s.
  - Visitors remove shoes and dress modestly (several travel sources; no temple-issued policy found).
- **Beaches.**
  - Uppuveli: about 3 km north (Wikivoyage) or "a few kilometres" (Rough Guides).
  - Nilaveli: about 15–16 km north, the launch point for Pigeon Island.
  - Marble Beach: near China Bay, run by the Sri Lanka Air Force (detachment since 2001, resort
    opened 2011), with a free public area.
- **Pigeon Island.**
  - About 1 km off Nilaveli. Sanctuary 1963, national park 2003 (the 17th); about 471 ha.
  - Rock pigeons breed on the islands.
  - Blacktip reef sharks; hawksbill, green and olive ridley turtles.
  - Damage from uncontrolled tourism. No-go zones cordoned off (2024 guide).
  - Crown-of-thorns and ghost-net clean-up in June 2025.
- **Whales.**
  - East-coast season about March–August (Sunday Times 2011), with most sightings March/April
    to August/September (Rough Guides).
  - Blue whales about 6–8 nautical miles east, around 30 minutes by boat; sperm whales in smaller
    numbers; spinner dolphins common.
  - Whales are occasionally seen from Swami Rock.
  - Sri Lanka Tourism refers to the Sea Mammals Observation, Regulation and Control Regulations.
- **War cemetery.** CWGC lists 314 identified Second World War casualties (other sources: 303).
  Originally the Combined Services Cemetery; became a permanent naval cemetery in April 1948.
- **Kanniya.**
  - Seven shallow square wells of varying temperature, about 8 km from town.
  - Declared archaeological protected monuments in 2011, with stupa and monastery ruins
    excavated from 2012.
  - Linked in legend to Ravana.
  - Bathing is by bucket.
- **Climate.**
  - Dry season April–August/September; wet season roughly October–March, with most rain October
    to January and November–December wettest (Wikipedia; Pigeon Island article: rain mostly
    October–March).
  - Beach villages are busiest May–September (Rough Guides).
- **Journeys.**
  - Sigiriya–Trincomalee about 99–100 km, about 2–3 h.
  - Polonnaruwa–Trincomalee about 107 km, about 2 h 10 min.
  - Kandy–Trincomalee about 4–5 h.
  - Trincomalee–Arugam Bay about 250 km, about 4–5 h 15 min.
  - Colombo–Trincomalee train about 7–8 h (07:25 day train; overnight mail train; Wikivoyage).

## Uncertain or conflicting
- Spotted deer in Fort Frederick: mentioned by Wikivoyage and widely by travellers; not found in
  an official source. The page says they "often graze"; remove if the owner prefers.
- Uppuveli and the tsunami: Rough Guides says Swami Rock sheltered Uppuveli, while another travel
  guide says the village was destroyed. The page avoids either claim.
- Marble Beach: Wikivoyage says it is inside a navy base with a fee; the Air Force runs it and
  describes a free public area. The page follows the Air Force.
- Pigeon Island fees (Sri Lanka Tourism lists US$10 adult, US$5 child, plus service and boat
  charges) look out of date and are not used. Whether the park closes in the north-east monsoon
  or boats are simply suspended is unclear; the page says boats can stop in rough weather.
- Airport–Trincomalee by road: one operator lists 548 km and 6 h 31 min, which is far too long;
  other figures suggest about 250–270 km. The page says "around 5–6 hours".
- Anuradhapura–Trincomalee: about 105–110 km; no reliable time found. The page says
  2.5–3 hours.
- Fort Frederick naming: the Dutch or the British, depending on the source. The page avoids it.
- Koneswaram’s age: 205 BCE vs 1580 BCE claims on the same official page. Not used.

## Experience copy suggestions
There are no Trincomalee entries in `src/data/experiences.mjs` yet. If the owner confirms
any, researched copy for possible cards:
- **Pigeon Island snorkelling** — `where`: Pigeon Island National Park, about 1 km off Nilaveli.
  `details`: Two small islands and their coral reef form a marine national park reached by
  boat from Nilaveli. Blacktip reef sharks and turtles are often seen in the shallows; don’t
  stand on coral or feed fish. Fees and rules are set by the Department of Wildlife
  Conservation, and boats may not run in rough weather.
- **Whale and dolphin watching** — `details`: From about March to August, boats head 6–8
  nautical miles offshore, about half an hour out, to look for blue whales, sperm whales and
  spinner dolphins. Trips leave early in the morning, and sightings are never guaranteed.
- **Koneswaram and Fort Frederick** — `details`: The clifftop Hindu temple on Swami Rock,
  rebuilt in the 20th century after the Portuguese destroyed the original in 1622, is reached
  through the gate of the old fort, still an army garrison. Remove shoes and cover shoulders
  and knees.
