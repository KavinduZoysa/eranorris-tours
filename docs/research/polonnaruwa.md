## Facts verified
- **History.**
  - A military post and provincial capital in the Anuradhapura period (CCF, Wikipedia).
  - The Cholas ruled from here and renamed it Jananathamangalam; the Siva Devale is attributed
    to Raja Raja I.
  - Vijayabahu I took the city in 1070.
  - Parakramabahu I reigned 1153–1186; Nissanka Malla 1187–1196 (Sunday Times).
  - Decline followed the Kalinga Magha invasion (1214/1215) and the 13th-century turmoil.
  - UNESCO 1982; CCF conservation since 1980.
- **Parakrama Samudra.** About 22.6 km², joining five tanks; Topa Wewa is older (about 386 AD).
- **Palace.** Described as seven storeys with a thousand rooms; the walls survive to about two
  storeys; brick with timber reinforcement (CCF).
- **Quadrangle.**
  - Vatadage: circular, two platforms, four seated Buddhas facing the entrances, a moonstone at
    the north entrance and guardstones (Wikipedia).
  - The Hatadage, opposite, was Nissanka Malla's Tooth Relic shrine.
  - The Gal Pota is a stone-book inscription.
  - The Nissanka Lata Mandapaya has lotus-stem pillars (CCF).
  - The Thuparama image house is a brick gedige (CCF).
- **Other monuments.**
  - Rankoth Vehera is 54 m high, the largest stupa, built under Nissanka Malla (Wikivoyage).
  - Kiri Vehera keeps its original lime plaster (Wikivoyage).
  - Lankatilaka and Tivanka have colossal brick Buddha images (CCF).
  - Tivanka holds most of the surviving Polonnaruwa-period paintings (CCF; Lonely Planet says the
    only surviving murals).
- **Gal Vihara** (Wikipedia):
  - Four images on one granite face: seated 4.6 m, cave figure 1.4 m, standing 6.9 m, reclining
    14.1 m.
  - A katikavata inscription.
  - The standing figure is debated (Ananda or the Buddha).
  - A modern shelter covers the images (History Hit and others).
- **Visiting.**
  - Tickets are sold at the museum, not at the entrance (Wikivoyage plus two trip reports).
  - They are checked at several points.
  - Bicycles are hired in town and at the entrance; the roads are flat.
  - Cover knees and shoulders.
  - Toque macaques live in the ruins (Wikipedia).
- **Nearby.**
  - Minneriya and Kaudulla elephants in the dry season (see the Sigiriya record).
  - Giritale tank built under Aggabodhi II (604–614).
- **Journeys.**
  - About 216 km from Colombo by road; train about 5 h.
  - Dambulla 66–67 km; Trincomalee 105–107 km, about 2–2.25 h.
  - Anuradhapura about 103 km; Sigiriya about 60 km.
  - Kandy about 150 km (4.5 h by bus).

## Uncertain or conflicting
- **When it became capital.** The Cholas ruled from here after 993/1017. Vijayabahu I took it in
  1070; CCF says "mid-11th century". The page states these events without one single date.
- **Reign dates.** CCF gives Vijayabahu I as 1055–1110 (his whole reign) and Nissanka Malla as
  "1167–1196", which looks like a typo; other sources give 1187–1196. The page uses 1187–1196.
- **Kalinga Magha invasion.** 1214 (Wikipedia, Polonnaruwa) vs 1215 (Wikipedia, Vatadage). The
  page says "from the 13th century".
- **Vatadage builder.** Parakramabahu I or Nissanka Malla. The page names neither.
- **Tivanka murals.** "Only surviving" (Lonely Planet) vs "mostly preserved in Tivanka", with
  similar paintings at Gal Vihara (CCF). The page says "most of the surviving".
- **Lankatilaka.** "Headless" Buddha image: CCF calls the images weathered, and a search extract
  suggested only a base survives. Check on site, or use "weathered".
- **Thuparama image house.** Often said to keep its original roof; not verified. The page says
  only "a vaulted brick shrine".
- **Potgul Vehera statue.** Its location is often described as near Potgul Vehera, south by the
  reservoir. Identification: king (Parakramabahu) or sage (CCF: "king or a sage").
- **Distances.**
  - Minneriya's entrance is about 25–30 km west on the Habarana road; no official figure was
    found.
  - The airport road time of 5–6 h is from Wikivoyage's car transfer and the distance;
    Kandy's 3.5–4 h by car is estimated from the 150 km route.
- **Round ticket.** As for Sigiriya: CCF lists it in 2026; Wikivoyage (2018) says it was
  discontinued.
- **Ticket office location.** All sources say the museum. Check whether there is now also a
  counter at the main gate.

## Rail after Cyclone Ditwah (added 2026-10-22)
- Lines to the north and east were suspended after Cyclone Ditwah (late 2025). Batticaloa and
  Trincomalee services resumed between December 2025 and January 2026. Northern Line works then
  limited services beyond Mahawa, and the Northern Line was reported reopening on 9 April 2026.
- The page gives the usual journey time and tells readers to check Sri Lanka Railways. Re-check
  before publishing.
