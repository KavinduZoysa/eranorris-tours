## Facts verified
- **History.**
  - Main port of the island from medieval times until Colombo's artificial harbour (breakwaters
    1870s–1880s; sources give 1873, 1875, 1884). The page says "late 19th century".
  - Portuguese fortified it in the late 16th century (fort 1588 per most sources; Portuguese
    presence and a chapel by 1541). Dutch captured it in 1640 and fortified it in stone from 1649;
    many walls 1663, sea wall completed 1729. British from 1796.
  - UNESCO inscription 1988; UNESCO stresses European planning combined with South Asian traditions;
    street-facing houses with verandas and internal courtyards.
  - Area inside the walls: 52 ha.
- **Ramparts.** Fourteen bastions (UNESCO nomination document; Dutch heritage page; Wikipedia). Circuit
  roughly 2–3 km (travel guides give 2 km and about 3 km). Western walls and Flag Rock are the usual
  sunset spots. Walking the walls and streets is free.
- **Lighthouse.** First lighthouse 1848 (British, iron); destroyed by fire (1934 or 1936); present
  26.5 m concrete tower built 1939 on Point Utrecht Bastion. Not open inside.
- **Gates.** Old Gate on the harbour side, Dutch, with VOC monogram and 1669 date; Main Gate opened by
  the British in 1873 between the Moon and Sun bastions.
- **Churches and mosque.** Dutch Reformed Church completed 1755 (begun 1752); floor paved with
  gravestones moved from the Dutch cemeteries in 1853. All Saints' (Anglican) 1871. Meeran Jumma
  Mosque 1904, near the lighthouse.
- **Museums.**
  - Maritime Archaeology Museum (CCF) in a Dutch warehouse of 1671/1672 above the Old Gate; opened
    1992; badly damaged in the 2004 tsunami; reopened 4 March 2010 with Dutch funding. Finds from
    the Avondster (1659) and Hercules (1661) wrecks.
  - National Museum of Galle (Dept. of National Museums) in a single-storey colonnaded building of
    1656, described as the oldest surviving Dutch building in the fort; opened 1986.
  - Old Dutch Hospital: rescue conservation in 2010 (GHF with ICOMOS Sri Lanka, World Monuments Fund);
    now shops and restaurants.
- **Rules.** Drones need Civil Aviation Authority permission; commercial filming needs GHF and
  Department of Archaeology permission (GHF page). Non-commercial photography is free.
- **Rumassala and Unawatuna.** Unawatuna about 5–6 km from Galle; sheltered crescent bay with a reef;
  calm for swimming mainly November–April. Peace pagoda built by Japanese Buddhist monks
  (Nipponzan Myohoji) in about 2005; Jungle Beach is a cove below Rumassala. Rumassala legend:
  part of a Himalayan mountain dropped by Hanuman (Ramayana).
- **Stilt fishermen** (Koggala, Ahangama): now mostly pose for paid photographs.
- **Climate.** SW monsoon May–September; inter-monsoon October–November; NE monsoon December–February;
  inter-monsoon March–April (DCS). The south-west coast is driest roughly December–April.
- **Journeys.**
  - Airport–Galle about 150 km, about 2–2.5 h via E03/E02/E01.
  - Colombo–Galle about 120 km; road about 1.5–2 h; train about 2 h scheduled (Rome2Rio),
    page says 2–3 h.
  - Galle–Hikkaduwa about 19 km (page: about 20 km, 30–45 min).
  - Galle–Bentota about 56 km (page: 55–65 km, 1–1.5 h).
  - Galle–Mirissa about 35 km (page: 45 min–1 h).
  - Galle–Tissamaharama/Yala about 142 km; Rome2Rio says 1 h 51 m, the page says 3–3.5 h to allow
    for the coast road at the end and stops.
  - Galle–Udawalawe about 115 km; Rome2Rio says 1 h 47 m, the page says 2.5–3 h.

## Uncertain or conflicting
- Bastion count: 14 (UNESCO document, Wikipedia) vs 12 (Sunday Observer). The page says fourteen.
- Lighthouse fire date: 1934 vs 1936. The page says only "replacing one lost to fire".
- Portuguese fortification date: 1541 chapel, 1587/1588 fort. The page says "late 16th century".
- Peace pagoda date: 2005 (Lonely Planet, Yamu) vs 2004 (Amazing Lanka). The page gives no date.
- Rampart circuit length: 2 km vs about 3 km. The page says "roughly 2–3 km".
- Rome2Rio journey times are well below real-world experience for inland legs; the page uses
  wider, longer ranges. These should be checked against the owner's own driving times.
- The UNESCO page itself could not be fetched (403); facts taken from search summaries and the
  nomination document.
