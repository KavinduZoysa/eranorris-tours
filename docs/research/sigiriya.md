# Research record — Sigiriya

Researched 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22 for `src/content/places/sigiriya.md`. Internal record, not published.

## Topics researched
Geology and height of the rock; Kassapa I and the chronicle account, alternative readings;
UNESCO status; the water, boulder and terrace gardens; the frescoes and the photography rule;
the Mirror Wall; the Lion Platform; the summit; the site museum; climb difficulty, steps and
time; bees and hornets; tickets, hours and the round ticket; drones; Pidurangala; Minneriya,
Kaudulla and Hurulu (the elephant "Gathering"); captive elephant rides; climate; road and rail
times to the airport, Dambulla, Polonnaruwa, Anuradhapura, Kandy and Trincomalee.

## Facts verified
- **Rock and history.**
  - Hardened magma plug of an extinct volcano (CCF, Wikipedia).
  - Monastic rock shelters from about the 3rd century BC (Rough Guides; Cobra Hood cave inscription).
  - Kassapa I, 477–495: killed his father Dhatusena, moved the capital from Anuradhapura,
    feared Moggallana, was defeated in 495; the rock then became a monastery until about the
    13th–14th century (CCF, Wikipedia, UNESCO).
  - Alternative readings (pleasure palace, Buddhist site) are noted on Wikipedia.
  - UNESCO inscription 1982.
- **Sights.**
  - Water gardens laid out symmetrically on an east–west axis, with moats, pools and fountains;
    some fountains work in the rainy season (CCF, Rough Guides, Wikipedia).
  - Boulder gardens with pavilion foundations and monks' caves; brick and limestone terraces.
  - Frescoes reached by metal spiral stairs; about 22 survive.
  - Mirror Wall of polished plaster with visitors' verses; writing on it is banned.
  - Lion Platform: only the paws remain; name from Sinhagiri.
  - Summit of about 1.6 ha with palace foundations and rock-cut cisterns (CCF, Wikipedia).
  - The site museum exists (Wikivoyage hours; Sunday Times).
- **Climb.**
  - About 1,200 steps (CCF and a second guide).
  - The whole visit takes 2–3 h (Rough Guides; CCF gives 1.5–2 h for the ascent).
  - The final iron staircase is exposed and hard for vertigo sufferers (Rough Guides).
  - Signs ask for quiet because of wasps (Wikivoyage). Mesh refuges stand on the Lion Platform
    (Rough Guides).
  - No refreshments inside; slippery when wet.
- **Rules.**
  - No photography at the frescoes (CCF announcement 2015; Lonely Planet).
  - Drones need prior written CCF approval (CCF tickets page).
- **Pidurangala.**
  - About 1 km north; monks moved here under Kassapa (Wikipedia).
  - A working temple at the base asks for modest dress, with scarves provided; a brick reclining
    Buddha stands partway up; the final boulder scramble needs hands; 20–45 min to the top;
    popular at sunrise (Wikivoyage, Jetwing, others).
- **Elephants.**
  - Minneriya and Kaudulla are linked by a corridor (Wikivoyage, Cinnamon/FT).
  - Elephants come for grass on the exposed tank beds in the dry season (former DWC Director
    General, Sunday Times).
  - Hurulu is the alternative when the other parks are not in season.
- **Climate.** Dry zone; most rain October–January; May is the hottest period (Wikipedia, Climate of Sri Lanka).
- **Journeys.**
  - Colombo–Sigiriya is about 167 km by road.
  - Taxi from Colombo 2 h 30 m–4 h 20 m; minivan from the airport 3 h 30 m–4 h 20 m (GeckoRoutes).
  - Dambulla is 17–18 km away (Wikivoyage); Polonnaruwa about 60 km.
  - Habarana station is about 15–18 km away.
  - Kandy is about 90 km, 2.5–3 h (Kandy research record).

## Uncertain or conflicting
- **Height.** UNESCO and Wikipedia give 180 m. CCF, the design and Wikivoyage give 200 m.
  Summit elevation is given as 349–370 m. The page says "nearly 200 m". In `verify:`.
- **Kassapa's dates.** 477–495 (UNESCO, CCF, Wikipedia). Rough Guides says the palace was built
  477–485 and that he died in 491. The page uses 477–495.
- **Surviving frescoes.** 22 (CCF, Wikipedia) vs 21 (Rough Guides). The page says "around twenty".
- **Mirror Wall verses.**
  - Counts: 685 deciphered (Paranavithana), "about 1,500" (Rough Guides), "over 1,800" (CCF).
  - Dates: 6th–14th century (CCF) vs mostly 9th–10th (Wikipedia).
  - The page gives no count, only "more than a thousand years ago".
- **Bees or hornets.** Rough Guides says bees; others say hornets or wasps. The page says "bees
  and hornets".
- **Closures.** Incident reports date from 2009–2014. One report said protective jackets became
  compulsory beyond the Lion Platform; this is not current and is not on the page.
- **Fresco photography ban.** Announced as "temporary" in 2015. Lonely Planet still lists it.
  Re-check on site.
- **Opening hours.** CCF ticket counters 7 am–5 pm; Wikivoyage gives 7:00–17:30; a third party
  gives 5:00–18:30. A 2023 CCF notice of 5:30 am sunrise opening now returns 404. The page
  quotes no hours.
- **Where tickets are sold.** Wikivoyage says foreigners buy them inside the museum; others say
  the ticket office by the car park. The page says "at the site or online".
- **Round ticket.** The CCF ticket page (2026) lists a Cultural Triangle Round Ticket. Wikivoyage
  (2018 data) says round tickets were discontinued. The page says the CCF "lists" one; confirm
  it is sold.
- **Museum ticket.** The CCF price list shows the Sigiriya Museum separately (USD 6). It is
  unclear whether the main ticket includes it. The page does not say.
- **Pidurangala.** The fee is reported as LKR 500 or 1,000. Climb time is 20–45 min.
- **Gathering season.** Sources give May–October, July–October, August–October (Kaudulla) or
  August–December. Counts may have fallen to under 200 in recent Septembers (Pilapitiya). The
  page says "roughly July to October, peaking around August and September".
- **Airport road time.** Only aggregator and operator estimates; the page says 3.5–4.5 hours.
- **Elephants on roads at dusk around Habarana.** Widely reported and in Wikivoyage's safety
  advice, but not from an official source.
- **Hydraulics.** "Wind-powered" or windmill hydraulics claims (Rough Guides, tourism sites) are
  speculative and are not used.

  - `where`: not needed (inside Sigiriya).
  - `details`: "The climb follows the old royal approach: through the water and boulder gardens,
    up a spiral staircase to the fresco gallery and the Mirror Wall, then past the giant lion's

    says "200 m" (see height above).
