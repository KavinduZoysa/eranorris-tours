## Facts verified
- **Setting.** About 98–100 km south of Colombo and 17–20 km north-west of Galle, on the A2
  Galle Road and the Colombo–Matara coastal railway. Sections north to south: town, reef and
  Turtle Beach, Wewala, Narigama, Thiranagama. Popular since the 1960s ("Hippiduwa"); Lonely
  Planet notes the northern beach is badly eroded.
- **National park.**
  - Wildlife sanctuary 18 May 1979 (Sri Lanka’s first marine sanctuary), nature reserve 1988,
    national park 19 September 2002. Managed by the Department of Wildlife Conservation.
  - About 101.6 ha; shallow fringing reef averaging about 5 m.
  - About 60 coral species and 170+ reef fish recorded; blacktip reef sharks on the outer
    slope; hawksbill, green and olive ridley turtles visit.
  - 1998 bleaching cut live coral cover from about 47% to 13%. In 2023 the park’s custodian
    put living coral at about 10% of its former level; restoration began at two sites.
  - Touching coral is prohibited; an offender was arrested, fined and ordered to restore
    damaged reef in 2025.
  - Glass-bottom boats are run locally and monitored by the DWC; reporting says they strike and
    damage the reef top.
- **Turtles.** Green turtles graze close inshore at the north end ("Turtle Beach"). Vendors sell
  seaweed for feeding; research elsewhere found tourist feeding changes turtles’ diet and
  behaviour. Collection of sea turtle eggs has been banned since 1972 (IOTN), and sea turtles
  are protected species.
- **Hatcheries.** Sri Lanka Tourism describes "several private turtle hatcheries" along the
  coast, which hold hatchlings in tanks and release them at 2–4 days old. IOTN reports they buy
  eggs from collectors. The page describes them neutrally as privately run, captive facilities.
- **Seasons and safety.**
  - Department of Census and Statistics (citing the Met Department): south-west monsoon
    May–September, north-east monsoon December–February, inter-monsoons March–April and
    October–November.
  - Sri Lanka Tourism: calm seas on the south-west coast November–March.
  - Coast Guard lifesavers are stationed at Hikkaduwa. Rescues were reported in October 2023
    and June 2022, and a tourist who ignored warning signs was swept away in January 2025.
- **Surf.** Season roughly November–April (Sri Lanka Tourism says November–March). Main Reef is
  a reef break; beginner waves and schools further south. Reef and rock hazards.
- **Diving.** Several wrecks (Sri Lanka Tourism says more than four); the *Conch*, a Shell oil
  tanker wrecked in 1903 on the Akurala reef.
- **Tsunami.** 26 December 2004: the *Queen of the Sea* (Matara Express) was destroyed at
  Peraliya. Estimates range from about 900 to 1,700+ dead, with around 1,000 often cited;
  Wikipedia calls it the largest rail disaster in history. Memorial at Peraliya; Tsunami
  Honganji Vihara Buddha statue about 30 m tall, donated from Japan; community museums at
  Peraliya/Telwatta, free or donation, with graphic photographs.
- **Nearby.** Ambalangoda about 15 km north (masks: sanni, kolam, raksha; kaduru wood; Ariyapala
  museum). Madu Ganga at Balapitiya: Ramsar site 1372 (2003, 915 ha), 15+ islands, Kothduwa
  temple reached by boat. Meetiyagoda moonstone pits between Ambalangoda and Hikkaduwa.
- **Journeys.**
  - Airport–Hikkaduwa about 2 h 10 min typical, 1 h 45 min–2 h 45 min range, via expressways
    and the Baddegama interchange.
  - Hikkaduwa–Galle about 20 km. Hikkaduwa–Mirissa about 52 km, roughly 55 min–1 h 30 min.
  - Hikkaduwa–Bentota about 37–39 km, about 40–45 min.

## Uncertain or conflicting
- Park area: 101.6 ha (Wikipedia, Protected Planet, CHM-CBD) vs 11.33 km² on one site. The page
  says "about 100 hectares".
- Coral species: 60 (Wikipedia) vs "about 70 varieties" (Hikkaduwa article). Not used on the page.
- Wikipedia’s park article gives the monsoon rains as April–June and September–November, which
  differs from the Met Department periods. The page uses the official seasons.
- Turtle-feeding ban: Wikivoyage says feeding is banned with heavy fines; no official notice
  found. The page advises against feeding without claiming a ban.
- Roped snorkelling and swimming zones: from Wikivoyage only. The page says "marked areas".
- Tsunami museums: two or three similar names (Tsunami Photo Museum, Telwatta; Community Tsunami
  Education Centre and Museum, Peraliya). Which one the villa site means is unknown.
- Tsunami death toll: estimates vary widely. The page says "well over a thousand ... thought to".
- Hikkaduwa–Yala time (3.5–4.5 h via the Southern Expressway) is an estimate from distance and
  expressway routing; no direct source found. Mirissa range (1–1.5 h) combines aggregator figures.

## Experience copy suggestions
For `src/data/experiences.mjs` (`where` only where the activity is outside Hikkaduwa itself).
These describe the activity in general and say nothing about what Eranorris includes.

- **hikkaduwa-cinnamon**
  - `where`: leave empty until the owner confirms where the garden is.
  - `details`: Sri Lanka’s “true” cinnamon is grown across the south-west. At a working garden you see the shoots cut, the outer bark scraped away and the inner bark loosened with a brass rod, then peeled in thin strips and rolled into quills to dry.
- **hikkaduwa-sea-turtles**
  - `where`: none (Turtle Beach, at the north end of the main beach).
  - `details`: Green turtles come into the shallows by the reef at the north end of the beach to graze on algae, often a few metres from swimmers. Watch from a distance and don’t touch, chase or feed them, even with the seaweed sold on the beach. Sightings are common in calm weather but never guaranteed.
- **hikkaduwa-turtle-hatchery**
  - `where`: leave empty until the owner confirms which hatchery.
  - `details`: Privately run hatcheries along this coast incubate eggs taken from nesting beaches before releasing the hatchlings, and keep turtles in tanks. It is a captive facility, not a wild encounter, and practices vary between hatcheries.
- **hikkaduwa-moonstone-mine**
  - `where`: Meetiyagoda, inland from Hikkaduwa towards Ambalangoda.
  - `details`: The moonstone pits at Meetiyagoda are narrow hand-dug shafts. Gravel and clay are hauled up in buckets and washed to find the stones, which are then cut and polished in nearby workshops, usually with a showroom attached.
- **hikkaduwa-rathgama-lagoon-kayaking**
  - `where`: Rathgama, just south of Hikkaduwa.
  - `details`: Rathgama Lagoon is a calm, shallow lagoon behind a narrow strip of land, fringed with mangroves and coconut palms. Early morning and late afternoon are cooler on the water, and kingfishers, egrets and water monitors are often seen, though not guaranteed.
- **hikkaduwa-tsunami-museum**
  - `where`: Peraliya or Telwatta, a few kilometres north of Hikkaduwa (confirm which museum).
  - `details`: Small community museums along the Galle Road remember the 2004 tsunami, which struck a crowded train at Peraliya; well over a thousand people are thought to have died. Photographs and personal accounts can be graphic. The railway memorial and a 30 m Buddha statue honouring the victims are nearby.
- **hikkaduwa-spice-garden**
  - `where`: leave empty until the owner confirms which garden.
  - `details`: Spice gardens on this coast grow cinnamon, pepper, cardamom, cloves, nutmeg and other spices used in Sri Lankan cooking and Ayurveda. A walk shows the plants and how each spice is harvested; many gardens have a shop.
- **hikkaduwa-madu-river-safari** (moved to Bentota on 2026-11-05 and merged into
  `bentota-river-safari`; the copy below was used there)
  - `where`: Madu Ganga at Balapitiya, about 20 km north; masks at Ambalangoda.
  - `details`: The Madu Ganga is a mangrove estuary scattered with islands, listed as a Ramsar wetland of international importance in 2003. Boats from Balapitiya wind through mangrove channels and usually stop at the temple on Kothduwa island. Ambalangoda, on the way, is known for carved masks used in healing rituals and folk theatre.
