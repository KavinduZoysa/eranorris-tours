## Facts verified
- **History.**
  - Capital from the 4th century BC (CCF; Wikipedia gives 437/377 BCE) for over 1,300 years.
  - Chola invasions of 993 and 1014–1017 led the capital to move to Polonnaruwa.
  - UNESCO 1982.
  - The New Town to the east dates from the 1949 preservation plan (Wikipedia).
  - The Mahavihara, Abhayagiri and Jetavana were rival fraternities, united under
    Parakramabahu I (Wikipedia, Jetavanaramaya).
- **Sri Maha Bodhi.** A cutting from the Bodh Gaya tree brought by Sanghamitta in the 3rd century
  BC. It is the oldest living planted tree with a known planting date (Wikipedia citing Guinness;
  CCF: "oldest documented tree"). It grows on a terrace about 6.5 m high with golden railings
  (1969). Security checks follow the 1985 attack.
- **Ruwanwelisaya.**
  - Begun by Dutugemunu around 140 BC.
  - Restoration society founded in 1902; final crowning in 2019.
  - About 103 m high (Wikipedia).
  - Elephant wall (two travel sources).
- **Thuparamaya.** Described as the first stupa built after Buddhism arrived, enshrining the
  right collarbone relic; ringed by pillars.
- **Lovamahapaya.** About 1,600 columns remain; it is called the Brazen Palace after its bronze
  roof tiles.
- **Abhayagiri.**
  - Founded by Valagamba around 89 BC; visited by Faxian in 412 CE.
  - Samadhi Buddha and Kuttam Pokuna are part of the complex.
  - The best-known moonstone is at "Mahasena's palace".
- **Jetavanaramaya.** Mahasena, 273–301. Originally 122 m high, now 71 m, built from about
  93.3 million bricks.
- **Isurumuniya.** Rock temple by Tissa Wewa, with carvings of the Lovers and the elephant pond.
  It charges its own fee (Wikivoyage).
- **Visiting.**
  - Tickets are checked between sites, and the old town closes to visitors at 17:00.
  - Remove shoes and hats; walk clockwise round stupas.
  - Pilgrims are white-clad; poya days are the busiest (Wikivoyage, plus two further guides on
    poya crowds).
- **Mihintale.**
  - About 12 km east.
  - Tradition places the meeting of Mahinda and Devanampiya Tissa here.
  - 1,840 steps, hospital ruins at the foot, Ambasthala, Maha Stupa and Aradhana Gala.
  - Busiest at Poson in June.
- **Aukana.** 5th century, 11.84 m, facing Kala Wewa.
- **Ritigala.**
  - About 43 km from Anuradhapura.
  - The mountain is a Strict Natural Reserve (DWC lists Ritigala as one of its three).
  - The monastery ruins are visited from the archaeology office; the CCF price list includes a
    Ritigala ticket.
- **Wilpattu.**
  - About 30 km west; the Hunuwilagama gate is off the A12.
  - The largest national park, with villus, leopards, sloth bears and elephants; jeep only.
- **Climate.** Dry zone; most rain October–January (Wikipedia).
- **Journeys.**
  - Colombo–Anuradhapura is about 194–206 km by road, about 3.5–4 h by car.
  - The train from Colombo takes about 4.5 h.
  - Sigiriya is about 2 h, Trincomalee about 107 km and Polonnaruwa about 103 km.

## Uncertain or conflicting
- **"First capital".** The design says "the island's first capital", and CCF calls it Sri Lanka's
  "first established kingdom". Sri Lanka Tourism says "third and the longest serving capital",
  and the chronicles name Tambapanni and Upatissa Nuwara earlier. The page avoids "first".
  In `verify:`.
- **Founding date.** 437 BCE (Mahavamsa) vs 377 BCE (Pandukabhaya) vs "4th century BCE" (CCF).
  The page says 4th century BC.
- **Bodhi tree planting date.** 288 BC, 249 BC or 236 BC. The page says 3rd century BC.
- **Ruwanwelisaya height.** 92–107 m across sources (103 m Wikipedia). The page says about 100 m.
- **End of the capital.** Dated 993 or 1017 (Chola invasions). The page says late 10th and
  early 11th centuries.
- **Jetavanaramaya's ranking.** It is called the third-tallest structure of the ancient world
  and the largest stupa by base area. The page says only "one of the tallest structures of the
  ancient world".
- **Ticket coverage.** Wikivoyage says the Sri Maha Bodhi and Ruwanwelisaya are free and the
  ticket covers the main archaeological sites, while Isurumuniya charges separately. The page
  says the CCF ticket covers the archaeological areas and museums and Isurumuniya has its own
  fee; it does not claim the Bodhi tree is free. Confirm.
- **Mihintale steps.** 1,840 (Wikipedia) vs 1,850 (Rough Guides). Distance 10–12.5 km.
- **Site museums at Abhayagiri and Jetavana.** Mentioned by guides (Wikivoyage lists the
  Archaeology and Folk museums). Not confirmed on an official page. Check the names.
- **Samadhi Buddha date.** Not verified (often given as the 3rd–4th century). The page gives no date.
- **Lovamahapaya columns.** One source says they date from a 12th-century rebuild. The page does
  not date them.
- **Isurumuniya Lovers.** The original slab may be displayed in the temple museum rather than
  on the rock. The page just says the carvings are there; check on site.
- **Vesak.** Usually May, occasionally June by the lunar calendar. The page says "usually in May".
- **Airport road time.** Operator estimates only; the page says 4–5 hours.

## Rail after Cyclone Ditwah (added 2026-10-22)
- Lines to the north and east were suspended after Cyclone Ditwah (late 2025). Batticaloa and
  Trincomalee services resumed between December 2025 and January 2026. Northern Line works then
  limited services beyond Mahawa, and the Northern Line was reported reopening on 9 April 2026.
- The page gives the usual journey time and tells readers to check Sri Lanka Railways. Re-check
  before publishing.
