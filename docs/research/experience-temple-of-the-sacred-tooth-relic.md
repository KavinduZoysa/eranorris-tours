goes deeper (history, the complex, the ceremonies, etiquette, access); the Kandy page keeps a

## Facts verified
- **History** (CCF):
  - The relic arrived in Sri Lanka in the 4th century CE, hidden in Princess Hemamala's hair.
  - "The capital city was wherever the relic resided." It reached Kandy in 1595.
- **Building** (CCF):
  - Vimaladharmasuriya I built a two-storey structure in the palace complex.
  - Kirti Sri Rajasinha and Sri Rajadhi Rajasinha expanded and decorated it.
  - Sri Vikrama Rajasinha added the octagonal Pattirippuwa as a royal audience pavilion. It
    now holds ola-leaf manuscripts.
- **Relic and shrine.** The relic is in seven nested golden caskets in an inner chamber entered
  only by custodians. Three daily ceremonies (dawn/morning, mid-morning, evening), when the
  doors open. Herbal bathing of the relic on Wednesdays (official FAQ).
- **Complex.** Alut Maligawa (a later shrine hall with seated Buddha images, and a museum).
  The ticket covers the Temple and the Dalada, World Buddhist and Tusker museums, plus the
  Royal Elephant Stable (official FAQ). The Magul Maduwa audience hall is in the palace
  grounds.
- **Visitor rules** (official FAQ):
  - Dress code; shoes and bags left at booths; security screening.
  - No plastic, no pets.
  - Machine-only tickets.
  - Access help (transport from the entrance, lift) via the administration office.
- **Attacks.** Damage in 1989 and 1998, restored each time (Britannica); "most notably in 1998" (CCF).

## Uncertain
- Who built the present structure. Sources credit Vimaladharmasuriya I for the first temple
  (about 1600), and Vira Narendra Sinha or 18th-century kings for the present form. The page
  avoids exact attributions beyond the octagon.
- Photography rules. The official FAQ doesn't state them; the page says to follow signs and
  staff instructions.
