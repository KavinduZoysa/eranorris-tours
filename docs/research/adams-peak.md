## Facts verified
- **Mountain.** 2,243 m. Footprint-shaped hollow about 1.8 m long. Buddhist (Buddha), Hindu
  (Shiva), Muslim (Adam) and Christian (St Thomas) traditions. Also called Samanalakanda; the
  god Saman is believed to protect climbers. Pilgrimage for over a thousand years; Ibn Battuta
  climbed it in 1344. Source of the Kelani, Kalu and Walawe rivers.
- **Protection.** Peak Wilderness Sanctuary since 1940 (DWC). One of the three components of
  the UNESCO Central Highlands of Sri Lanka (2010). IUCN 2025 outlook: "significant concern";
  waste and visitor pressure among the threats.
- **Season.**
  - Opens on Unduvap Poya (December full moon). The 2024 season began on 14 December 2024 and
    the 2025 season on 4 December 2025.
  - Closes on Vesak Poya (May).
  - Path lit and stalls open in season. Busiest on poya days, weekends and holidays;
    April is the peak pilgrimage month.
- **Dalhousie route.**
  - Start at about 1,250 m; about 1,000 m gain.
  - About 5–7 km each way (sources differ); about 5,000–5,500 steps.
  - About 3–4 h up and 2–3 h down; 1.30–2.30 am starts for a 6–6.30 am sunrise.
  - Landmarks: Peace Pagoda about halfway (built 1978); Seetha Gangula stream; Indikatu
    Pahana; Mahagiridamba, the steepest stretch, with rails on both sides.
- **Ratnapura–Palabaddala.** Much longer: about 15 km, about 7 h (Rough Guides).
- **Shadow.** A triangular shadow is cast west at sunrise on clear mornings, for a short time.
- **Etiquette.** Greeting and distance etiquette and the bell come from Sri Lanka Tourism.
  Modest dress, removing shoes at the summit and keeping noise down come from travel guides and
  a pilgrim’s account; there is no official etiquette text.
- **Plastics.** The Cabinet approved a ban in the Sri Pada area on high-density polythene
  wrappers and bags, sachets, polystyrene, single-use cups and cutlery, and single-use plastic
  bottles under 1 litre. Businesses must remove their own waste. Effective for the 2025–26
  season ("Muni Siripa – Green Pilgrimage").
- **Off-season.** Lights off, stalls closed, heavy rain, wind and mist, leeches (sripada.org,
  Rough Guides).
- **Ditwah and landslides.**
  - Ditwah damaged the Mahagiridamba steps in late 2025. The Army and Navy repaired them, and
    the NBRO declared the Hatton route safe on 9 January 2026.
  - On 6 August 2026 a landslide near Ehela Kanuwa blocked the steps to the upper shrine on the
    Nallathanni route. Police said off-season visitors could use the Ratnapura route via
    Idikatu Pahana; no clearance date was given.
- **Access.**
  - Hatton–Dalhousie about 32 km via Dickoya, Norwood and Maskeliya; about 1–1.5 h by road
    (bus 1–2 h).
  - Colombo–Dalhousie about 4–5 h. Kandy–Dalhousie 2.5–3 h (one tour operator); the page says
    3–4.5 h to allow for hill roads.
  - St Clair’s Falls is 5 km west of Talawakele on the Hatton road; Devon Falls has a viewpoint
    near Talawakele.

## Uncertain or conflicting
- Season start: most sources (and the 2024 and 2025 openings) say Unduvap Poya in December;
  Rough Guides says Duruthu Poya (December/January). The sripada.org trail page says
  December–April. The page uses Unduvap to Vesak.
- Route length: 5 km (sripada.org header) vs 7 km (Rough Guides); sripada.org also says "12 km"
  elsewhere. The page says "about 5–7 km".
- Step count: 5,200–5,500 in different sources. The page says "around 5,000–5,500".
- Climb time: 3 h to 4–6 h up in different sources. The page says "about 3–4 hours" for fit
  walkers, longer on busy nights.
- "January to March usually brings the clearest dawns" is inferred from climate normals for the
  hill country and from guide sources. There is no official statement.
- Kandy–Dalhousie and Nuwara Eliya–Dalhousie times are estimates built from leg times.
- Whether the August 2026 landslide debris has been cleared before the 2026–27 season.
- Whether the plastic rules continue unchanged for 2026–27.

## Experience copy suggestions
**adams-peak-sunrise-climb** (Overnight sunrise climb)
- `where`: Starts at Dalhousie (Nallathanni), about 32 km from Hatton
- `details`: The Dalhousie route climbs about 1,000 m on several thousand steps, lit all night
  during the December–May pilgrimage season. Most people set off between about 1.30 and 2.30 am
  to reach the summit shrine for sunrise, taking around 3–4 hours up and 2–3 hours down. Bring
  warm layers and a refillable bottle: small plastic bottles and polythene are banned in the
  Sri Pada area.
