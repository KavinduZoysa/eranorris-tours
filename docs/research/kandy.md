## Facts verified
- **Status and history.**
  - Last royal capital until the British took Kandy in 1815.
  - UNESCO World Heritage "Sacred City of Kandy", inscribed 1988.
  - Elevation about 500 m (Britannica; other sources give 465–500 m).
- **Lake.** Created under Sri Wickrama Rajasinghe from about 1807 (sources give 1807–1812) by
  flooding paddy fields. The Walakulu Bamma parapet was left unfinished in 1815.
- **Temple** (official FAQ):
  - Open 5:30 am–8 pm. Puja services early morning, mid-morning and evening.
  - No shorts, sleeveless or revealing clothing; suitable clothing at the entrances.
  - Footwear and bulky bags left at reception booths, with a separate booth for foreign
    tourists. Security screening; plastic bags discouraged.
  - Tickets only from the on-site machines. The ticket covers the Temple and the Dalada,
    World Buddhist and Tusker museums, plus the Royal Elephant Stable.
- **Perahera** (official page):
  - Annual, in Esala.
  - The 2026 schedule had 5 Kumbal and 5 Randoli nights (18–27 August), the water-cutting
    ceremony, then a day procession on 28 August.
  - Dates follow the lunar calendar and are set by the temple.
- **Peradeniya** (official):
  - About 5.5 km west of Kandy on the Colombo road, beside the Mahaweli River.
  - 147 acres, about 460 m. A royal garden under King Kirti Sri; formed as a botanic garden
    in 1821 by Alexander Moon.
  - Orchid house, spice garden, royal/cabbage/palmyrah palm avenues, Java fig on the Great Lawn.
- **Kandyan dance.** The ves form derives from the Kohomba kankariya ritual. Visitor shows run
  about an hour; seating is often first-come.
- **Udawattakele.** Forest reserve from 1856, sanctuary from 1938, about 104 ha, on the ridge
  behind the temple.
- **Temple loop.** Gadaladeniya (stone), Lankatilaka (brick) and Embekke (wood carvings), from
  the 14th-century Gampola period, a few km west of Kandy.
- **Pinnawala.**
  - Set up in 1975 by the Wildlife Department, taken over by the National Zoological Gardens
    in 1978.
  - Captive breeding since 1982.
  - Elephants bathed in the Maha Oya.
- **Climate.** Driest roughly January–March/April; wettest roughly October–December. Affected
  by both monsoons, less than the coasts.
- **Journeys.**
  - Airport–Kandy about 1 h 50 m–2 h 30 m via the Central Expressway, traffic permitting
    (transfer operator). The page says "around 2–3 hours".
  - Colombo–Kandy train about 2.5–3.5 h scheduled, often delayed.
  - Kandy–Sigiriya about 90 km, 2.5–3 h.
  - Kandy–Nuwara Eliya about 80 km, about 3 h.
  - Kandy–Ella train 6–7 h scheduled, often 7+; book ahead.

## Rail disruption (added 2026-10-22)
Cyclone Ditwah (late 2025) damaged the Main Line in many places.
- Badulla–Ambewela reopened in December 2025, and Nanu Oya–Badulla (via Ella) on 20 June
  2026.
- As of October 2026, Peradeniya–Kandy was still suspended (Peradeniya bridge works), and
  so were sections between Gampola and Nanu Oya. Colombo–Peradeniya/Gampola was reported
  running.
- Full restoration is targeted for the end of 2026.
- The page says "at the time of writing (October 2026)". Re-check before publishing.
