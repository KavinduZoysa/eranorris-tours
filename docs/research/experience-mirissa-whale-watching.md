## Topics researched
Species off Mirissa and how often they are seen; why whales are close to shore (shelf and slope);
season and sea conditions; what a typical trip involves (departure, duration, boat types, permit);
Sri Lanka's whale-watching law (2012 regulations) and the 2026 SLTDA draft criteria; crowding and
compliance problems; choosing an operator; seasickness and comfort; the east-coast alternative.

## Facts verified
- **Species** (Integrative Zoology 2021; boat surveys January–May 2017, 55 days):
  - 242 sightings of at least 9 species.
  - Baleen whales: blue 167 of 174 encounters, Omura's 4, Bryde's 3.
  - Toothed whales and dolphins: spinner dolphin 28, sperm whale 18, common bottlenose dolphin 13,
    short-finned pilot whale 5, melon-headed whale 2, killer whale.
  - Blue and sperm whales favoured the outer shelf and slope; the authors call for better management.
- **Encounter rates.** Operator records 2008–2015 (992 sailings) show monthly blue-whale encounter
  rates mostly 70–90%. Not collected to a scientific protocol; may over-represent sailings with
  sightings. The page says "Operators' own records suggest whales are found on most trips in the
  main months" and "no trip can promise a sighting".
- **Season.** About November–April at Mirissa; few trips May–October (SW monsoon). Navy: Galle
  November–April, Trincomalee May–October.
- **Trip.** Departures about 6–7 am; typically 3–5 h at sea, longer packages exist; morning seas often
  calmer. Boats range from converted fishing boats to double-deckers (IWC).
- **2012 regulations.**
  - Vessels licensed by the DWC Director-General; certificate of fitness; insurance covering crew
    and passengers; life jackets and life-saving equipment; GPS and communications.
  - Operator, assistant operator and a DWC-trained, registered guide on board.
  - Each person or group needs a permit, issued at the departure point, for a fee.
  - Observation only between 5.30 am and 7 pm.
  - Gradually slow down on sighting until 400 m. For whales, engine off at 100 m and never closer
    than 100 m; other sea mammals no closer than 50 m.
  - No abrupt changes of speed or direction; do not position in front of or behind the animals; do
    not block migratory routes.
  - No artificial food, lights or sounds to attract the animals. No liquor on board; no boarding
    while intoxicated.
  - Passengers not to get into the sea or do anything harmful, except diving with the
    Director-General's prior approval.
  - Do not go out in bad weather; return immediately if the weather turns.
  - Passengers to be briefed on conservation and the restrictions before departure.
- **SLTDA draft criteria (2026, not yet final).**
  - Swimming or diving with whales or dolphins strictly prohibited; no physical interaction or
    feeding.
  - Life jackets worn by all passengers and crew at all times on board.
  - Pre-departure briefing, including seasickness awareness and expected sea conditions.
  - Speed no more than 5 knots in the caution zone; no head-on approaches.
  - Drones not below 500 m within a 500 m radius.
  - No paddle craft or jet skis for whale watching.
  - Clear pricing, duration and cancellation/refund terms before booking.
- **Crowding.** Accounts of 17 boats around a single whale; reports of boats approaching within a few
  metres despite the 100 m rule; unregistered boats and untrained guides reported.
- **Seasickness** (NHS): sit where movement is least (middle of a boat); look at the horizon; fresh
  air; avoid screens, heavy meals and alcohol shortly before; remedies from a pharmacist; ginger.

## Uncertain or conflicting
- Whether the 2012 regulations have been amended since. A blog claims a 2026 "Marine Sanctuary
  Protection Act" with 300 m and 100 m zones and LKR 500,000 fines; not corroborated, so not used.
- The SLTDA criteria are a draft. The page does not cite them, but its life-jacket and swimming
  advice is consistent with them.
- Reports (The Island) of DWC restrictions on Mirissa vessels and requests to lift them: details not
  found.
- Peak months: sources give December–March, February–March or "mid-November to early April". The page
  avoids naming a peak.
- Whether the wildlife permit is usually bundled into operator prices: unknown; the page tells
  guests to ask.
- Typical distance offshore: not stated in an official source. The page says only "a short run
  offshore" and "an hour or more" to deep water.
