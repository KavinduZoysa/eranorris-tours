## Facts verified
- **Setting.** About 1,868 m (Tea Board, Sri Lanka Tourism, Wikipedia). Pidurutalagala, the
  highest mountain, is 2,524 m; its summit carries military and telecoms installations and access
  is restricted.
- **History.** Plateau found by a British hunting party in 1818 (John Davy). Governor Edward Barnes
  built a residence, now the Grand Hotel. Samuel Baker founded a settlement in 1846 with English
  livestock and vegetables. Coffee failed after the 1870s–80s blight; tea plantations established
  by 1875 and widespread by 1900. Post office built 1894.
- **Tea.** Highest average elevation of the tea districts; pale, golden, fragrant teas (Tea Board).
  Factory tours cover plucking, withering, rolling, fermentation, firing, grading (Central
  Province tourism, Labookellie). Pedro is about 3.5 km from town, with a short factory tour; no
  photography inside; processing mostly at night (Lonely Planet, Yamu).
- **Horton Plains.**
  - Sanctuary 1969, national park 1988; managed by the DWC; part of the UNESCO Central
    Highlands serial site (with Peak Wilderness and Knuckles), inscribed 2010.
  - Plateau roughly 2,100–2,300 m. About 9–9.5 km loop, about 3 hours.
  - World’s End drop 870–880 m (sources vary). Baker’s Falls about 20 m.
  - Mist by mid-morning, especially April–September; clearest January–March.
  - Sambar deer common; leopards present but rarely seen; endemic birds.
  - Polythene banned (sambar deaths from plastic). Sundays and holidays busy (Lonely Planet).
  - Day-visitor permits through the DWC e-permit system (printed receipt with QR code advised).
- **Around town.**
  - Gregory Lake created by Sir William Gregory by damming the Thalagala/Nanu Oya streams.
  - Galway’s Land NP: about 2 km east; sanctuary 1938, NP 2006; a birding site, as is Victoria Park.
  - Hakgala (official): 1861 for cinchona; about 1,745 m; 28 ha; 9.5 km south-east on the
    Badulla road; flowers best mid-March to end of April; open 7.30 am–6 pm, tickets until 5 pm.
  - Seetha Amman Temple near Hakgala, a Ramayana site.
  - St Clair’s (5 km west of Talawakele) and Devon Falls (viewpoint at the 20th milepost) are on
    the Hatton–Talawakele road (Sri Lanka Tourism).
- **Climate** (WMO 1991–2020 normals via Wikipedia):
  - Mean highs 18.6–22.5 °C; mean lows 10.2–13.8 °C.
  - Record lows below zero in December–March.
  - Driest February–March (about 72 mm); wettest October (247 mm) and November (226 mm).
  - Rain 130–170 mm a month May–September.
- **April season.** Busiest around the Sinhala and Tamil New Year, with horse racing (Governor’s Cup),
  motor racing, golf and a flower show.
- **Rail.**
  - Nanu Oya station about 8 km from town.
  - Nanu Oya–Badulla reopened 20 June 2026 (four trains a day, per the Transport Minister).
  - As of 21 October 2026 (blog), Peradeniya–Kandy, Gampola–Nawalapitiya and Hatton–Nanu Oya
    were still closed for repair; no through train from Kandy.
- **Journeys (estimates).**
  - Kandy–Nuwara Eliya 75–80 km, about 2.5–3 h.
  - Airport–Nuwara Eliya about 4–5 h (transfer operators); the page says 4.5–6 h.
  - Nuwara Eliya–Ella about 60 km (route planners say 1.5 h, which is optimistic for hill roads);
    the page says 2–3 h.
  - Nuwara Eliya–Dalhousie via Talawakele and Hatton: no direct source; built from
    Hatton–Dalhousie (about 1–1.5 h) plus Nuwara Eliya–Hatton. The page says 2.5–3.5 h.

## Uncertain or conflicting
- Elevation: the design’s highlight says 1,900 m; official sources say 1,868 m. The page uses 1,868 m.
- Davy’s discovery 1818 vs 1819; Baker 1846 vs 1847. The page follows the Tea Board (1818) and
  Wikipedia (1846).
- Gregory Lake: 1873 (Wikipedia) vs 1874 (Central Province tourism). The page says "the 1870s".
- World’s End drop: 870 m, 880 m and "about 900 m". The page says "roughly 870–880 m".
- Dry season: the Tea Board says February–April; the normals and the national park sources say
  January–March, and April is wetter (134 mm). The page says January–March.
- Horton Plains opening hours: 6 am–6 pm (third-party) vs narrower reseller hours. Not stated on
  the page; it links the DWC.
- Pidurutalagala summit access: accounts conflict, so the page says only "a restricted area".
- "Some factories process at night" comes from Lonely Planet and may vary by factory and season.
- Rail status: only one dated source (a blog) for October 2026. Official confirmation is
  needed before publishing.

## Experience copy suggestions
**nuwara-eliya-tea-factory** (Tea estate and factory visit)
- `where`: Estates around Nuwara Eliya and along the Kandy road
- `details`: Nuwara Eliya has the highest average elevation of Sri Lanka’s tea districts, and
  its estates are known for a pale, fragrant tea. A factory tour follows the leaf through
  withering, rolling, fermenting, firing and grading, and usually ends with a tasting. Much of
  the processing happens at night, so the machines may be idle by day, and some factories do
  not allow photography inside.
