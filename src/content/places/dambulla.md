## A cave temple still in use

The caves sit high on a granite outcrop that rises about 160 m above the plain on the edge of Dambulla town. Tradition says King Valagamba (Vattagamani Abhaya) sheltered here during an exile in the 1st century BC and turned the caves into a temple when he regained the throne. Later kings added to it. Nissanka Malla had statues gilded in the 12th century, and the Kandyan kings restored and repainted the caves in the 18th century, so much of the painting you see today dates from that period.

UNESCO lists the Golden Temple of Dambulla as a World Heritage Site. It counts more than 150 statues and about 2,100 m² of murals across the caves. It is also a working monastery, the Rangiri Dambulla Raja Maha Viharaya, and you will share the caves with monks and worshippers carrying flowers.

## Inside the five caves

The caves open off a long covered terrace along the rock face, and most visitors walk through them in order.

- **Cave 1, Devaraja Viharaya.** A 14 m reclining Buddha cut from the rock fills much of the cave, with a figure of his disciple Ananda nearby.
- **Cave 2, Maharaja Viharaya.** The largest cave is about 52 m wide. It holds more than fifty Buddha statues, standing and seated, with statues of King Valagamba and King Nissanka Malla. Scenes from the Buddha's life and the island's history are painted across the rock ceiling. Water drips from a crack in the roof into a pot and is said to have healing powers.
- **Cave 3, Maha Alut Viharaya.** The "Great New Temple" dates from the Kandyan period. It has around fifty Buddha images, a statue of King Kirti Sri Rajasinha and elaborate paintings in the later Kandyan style.
- **Caves 4 and 5.** These are smaller shrines. The fourth has a seated Buddha under a carved arch and a small stupa. The fifth, the most recent, has reclining Buddhas and images of Hindu deities, a reminder of how the two traditions have mixed here.

The light inside is dim, and your eyes take a minute to adjust. Look up as often as you look around: much of the painting is overhead.

## The climb and visiting rules

The path climbs the rock's gentler slope on stone steps. Most people take 10–20 minutes, longer in the midday heat. The views across the forest reach as far as Sigiriya on a clear day.

- **Tickets first.** Buy your ticket at the counter at the bottom before you climb, as there is nowhere to buy one at the top. Hours are on the Central Cultural Fund's ticket page, linked below.
- **Dress.** Cover your shoulders and knees, and take off your hat at the shrines. Visitors in shorts or sleeveless tops may be refused entry.
- **Shoes.** You leave them before the cave terrace. The rock and the terrace floor get very hot by midday, so light socks help.
- **Photography.** Flash is not allowed. Don't pose with your back to a Buddha image, and ask before photographing monks or worshippers.
- **Monkeys.** Macaques live on the rock. Keep food out of sight and hold on to bags.

## The Golden Temple at the foot of the hill

Beside the main road, a modern temple and Buddhist museum building is topped by a large golden seated Buddha. The name "Golden Temple" is often used for the whole site, so it is easy to confuse the two. The historic caves are the climb above it.

## Around Dambulla

- **Ibbankatuwa megalithic tombs.** A short drive south-west on the Kurunegala road, stone-slab graves about 2,700 years old have yielded pottery, beads and cremated remains. A visit takes about half an hour.
- **Popham's Arboretum.** On the Kandalama road, Sam Popham began restoring this small patch of dry-zone forest in 1963. Marked trails make a quiet hour's walk, and night walks look for slender lorises.
- **The wholesale market.** Dambulla is the hub of the island's vegetable trade, and its large wholesale market shows a side of the town most visitors drive past.
- **On the Kandy road.** Nalanda Gedige is a stone shrine built between the 8th and 10th centuries, which mixes a Hindu temple plan with Buddhist use. Near Matale, the Aluvihare cave temple is linked with the first writing down of the Buddhist scriptures. Spice gardens line the same road. Entry is usually free, but buying is expected, often at high prices.
- **Aukana.** The 5th-century standing Buddha at Aukana, nearly 12 m tall and carved from the living rock, faces the Kala Wewa reservoir. It is an easy detour on the road to [Anuradhapura](/places/anuradhapura/).

## How long to spend

The caves take **1–2 hours**, including the climb. **Half a day** lets you add Ibbankatuwa or the arboretum. Few travellers stay in Dambulla itself. [Sigiriya](/places/sigiriya/) is close, so most see the caves from there or on the way between Kandy and Sigiriya.

A typical half-day from Sigiriya:

- **Mid-afternoon:** the 30-minute drive, then the climb and the five caves, unhurried.
- **Late afternoon:** the Ibbankatuwa tombs or the arboretum, then back to Sigiriya by dusk.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport.** Dambulla is about 150 km from Colombo, roughly 3–4 hours by road via Kurunegala, depending on traffic.
- **From Kandy.** It is about 72 km north on the main road through Matale, usually 2–2.5 hours.
- **Onward.** Sigiriya is about 17–20 km away, around 30 minutes. [Polonnaruwa](/places/polonnaruwa/) is about 1.5 hours east, and Anuradhapura 1.5–2 hours north.

The town is a major junction, so central traffic can be slow. In a private tour you add Dambulla to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates.

## Best time to visit

The caves are worth seeing in any season. Like the rest of the Cultural Triangle, Dambulla gets most of its rain from about October to January. The climb is hottest from late morning to mid-afternoon, which is also when tour groups arrive, so aim for early morning or late afternoon.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the Dambulla cave temple take?

Allow 1–2 hours, including 10–20 minutes for the climb each way and time inside the five caves.

### Is the climb to the Dambulla caves difficult?

No. It is a steady walk up stone steps on the rock's gentle slope. Take it slowly in the heat and carry water.

### Where do I buy tickets?

At the counter at the foot of the rock, before you climb. There is nowhere to buy one at the cave entrance. Check the Central Cultural Fund's ticket page for current hours.

### Is the Golden Temple the same as the cave temple?

The name is used for both. The building with the large golden Buddha beside the road is a modern addition at the foot of the hill. The historic painted caves are at the top of the climb.

### Can I visit Dambulla and Sigiriya on the same day?

Yes. They are about 30 minutes apart. Climb Sigiriya early in the morning and visit the caves in the afternoon, or the other way round.
