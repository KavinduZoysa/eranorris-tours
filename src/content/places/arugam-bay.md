## A surf town at the end of the road

Arugam Bay lies on the south-east coast in Ampara District, about 320 km from Colombo and a few kilometres south of the market town of Pottuvil. The town is essentially one road lined with guesthouses, surf shops and cafés, behind a curved sandy bay that ends in a rocky headland, the Main Point. A lagoon lies behind the bay, and inland the dry-zone scrub and forest is elephant country.

Fishing was the livelihood here long before surfing, and the area is home to Muslim, Tamil and Sinhalese communities. The 2004 tsunami destroyed much of the bay and of Pottuvil. Since then surf tourism has grown quickly, but the place is still strongly seasonal: from about November to March many guesthouses and cafés close. Beachwear is fine on the sand; in Pottuvil and the villages, cover shoulders and knees.

## The surf: points and season

Sri Lanka Tourism gives the surf season as April to October, when the south-west monsoon winds blow offshore on this coast. Swell and crowds are usually biggest around July and August. Most breaks are right-hand points spread along some 30 km of coast:

- **Main Point**, at the south end of the bay, can be fast and hollow and suits experienced surfers; its gentler inside section, Baby Point, is where many lessons happen.
- **Pottuvil Point**, north of Pottuvil, is a long wave along a boulder-strewn beach, favoured by intermediates.
- **Elephant Rock** and **Peanut Farm**, south of town, are softer on small days and popular with learners and surf schools.
- **Okanda**, roughly 25 km south near Kumana, is more remote and better for strong surfers.
- **Lighthouse**, well north of Pottuvil, is quieter, with waves for different levels, for those who want to escape the crowds.

Lessons and board hire are easy to find in season. On a tour with us, we arrange surf lessons if you ask, and they are included in your tour price. Board hire for surfing on your own is not included. On a tour with us, we can carry your own surfboard on a roof rack. Tell us when you plan your tour, so we send the right vehicle. Several breaks have reef or boulders underneath and busy line-ups in peak months, so start where your level fits and ask locally about the day’s conditions. Sandbanks shift, too: Whiskey Point, north of town, was long recommended for learners but has been unreliable since erosion damaged it. Crocodiles live in the lagoons beside Pottuvil Point, Peanut Farm and Elephant Rock, and wild elephants cross the road south of town, so don’t walk or ride a scooter there after dark.

## Swimming and the sea

Arugam Bay is a surf beach first. The calmer water is usually in the bay away from the breaking waves, but currents run along this coast and a lifeguard may not be on duty. Swim within your depth, keep clear of surfers and the points, and ask locally before going in. From about November the north-east monsoon brings onshore wind, rain and rougher seas.

## Lagoons, elephants and Kumana

- **Lagoons.** Arugam Lagoon behind the bay and Pottuvil Lagoon to the north are fringed with mangroves and good for birds. Boat trips on Pottuvil Lagoon, usually early or late in the day, wind through the mangroves past herons, kingfishers and monitor lizards; elephants sometimes come to the water’s edge. On a tour with us, the lagoon boat trip is included in your tour price. Your driver takes you to the boat for the early start. Crocodiles live in both lagoons, so keep out of the water there.
- **Elephant Rock.** A short ride south of town and a sandy walk bring you to this boulder above the beach, with wide views over the coast and lagoon at sunset. Leave before dark, when elephants move through the scrub.
- **Elephants.** Wild elephants move between Lahugala and Kumana. Herds gather at the tanks of Lahugala Kitulana National Park, about 16 km inland on the Monaragala road, especially in the dry months, and elephants are sometimes seen near Elephant Rock and on the Panama road. Watch from a distance and never feed them; sightings are never guaranteed. On a tour with us, your driver can take you to Lahugala if you ask. The drive from Ella passes Lahugala, so your driver can also stop there on the way in.
- **Kumana National Park**, about 40 km south via Panama and Okanda, adjoins Yala and is known above all for birds: from about April to July, tens of thousands of waterbirds gather to nest at the Kumana Villu swamp. Elephants, crocodiles and occasionally leopards are seen too. Visitors need a permit and a guide from the park office at Okanda, and go in by jeep. On a tour with us, the jeep, the permit and the park guide are included in your tour price. The jeep picks you up at your hotel in Arugam Bay. Parks close at short notice for drought or heavy rain: Kumana shut in the late-November rains of 2025, and its campsites closed for drought in September 2026. Check before you go.

## Temples and history

- **Muhudu Maha Vihara**, on the beach at Pottuvil, is an ancient Buddhist site. Legend links it to Queen Viharamahadevi, mother of King Dutugemunu, said to have come ashore on this coast; granite statues stand among the ruins.
- **Kudumbigala**, a forest hermitage near Panama, has more than 200 rock caves once used by monks, and a stupa on top of a rock reached by steps cut into the stone. The climb is steep and exposed, so skip it in rain. It is still a place of meditation: dress modestly and keep your voice down. On a tour with us, your driver can take you to Kudumbigala if you ask. You can add Okanda on the same trip.
- **Magul Maha Vihara**, near Lahugala, is the ruin of an ancient monastery in the forest.
- **Okanda**, by the Kumana park office, has a Hindu shrine to Murugan. Each year, usually in June or July, thousands of pilgrims gather here before walking through Kumana and Yala to Kataragama, when the park gate is opened for them.

## When to go

- **About April to October** is the season: dry weather, offshore winds and surf. July and August are busiest, so book early. October and early November can still bring good days, especially at Okanda, with fewer people.
- **About November to March** brings the north-east monsoon, with rain, onshore winds and many places closed. At that time of year the south-west coast, around [Hikkaduwa](/places/hikkaduwa/) and [Mirissa](/places/mirissa/), is in season instead.

## How long to stay

**Two or three nights** give you a lesson or a few sessions, a lagoon or Kumana trip and time on the beach. Because the drive from anywhere is long, one night rarely feels worthwhile. Surfers often stay a week or more. On a tour with us, your driver stays nearby and takes you on day trips around Arugam Bay, also on longer surf stays.

A typical day:

- **Dawn:** surf, or watch the fishing boats come in; the waves are often cleanest early.
- **Late morning:** breakfast and shade through the heat.
- **Afternoon:** a lagoon trip, or a drive south to Kudumbigala or Kumana.
- **Evening:** a last session or a walk along the bay, then dinner on the main road.

## Getting there and moving on

Arugam Bay is a long way from everywhere, which is part of its appeal.

- **From the airport or Colombo.** About 7–8 hours by road, longer with stops. Many travellers break the journey in the hills or near Yala. On a tour with us, we can add a night in the hills or near Yala to break the drive.
- **From Ella.** [Ella](/places/ella/) is about 3–4 hours away via Wellawaya and Monaragala, the usual link with the hill country.
- **From Yala.** [Yala](/places/yala/) is about 3–4 hours via Buttala and Monaragala.
- **Up the east coast.** [Trincomalee](/places/trincomalee/) is about 6–7 hours, via Batticaloa.

In a private tour you add Arugam Bay to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the surf season in Arugam Bay?

From about April to October, when the wind blows offshore on this coast. July and August usually bring the biggest swell and the biggest crowds; from November to March the surf is poor and many places close.

### Is Arugam Bay good for beginner surfers?

Yes, in parts. Main Point suits experienced surfers, but its inside section and the softer breaks to the south are used for lessons. Take a lesson first and ask which break suits the day. On a tour with us, the lesson is included in your tour price.

### Can I swim at Arugam Bay?

Yes, with care. Swim in the calmer water of the bay, away from the points and surfers, stay within your depth and don’t assume a lifeguard is on duty. Keep out of the lagoons, where there are crocodiles.

### How far is Kumana National Park from Arugam Bay?

About 40 km south via Panama and Okanda, usually an hour or more by road. Visitors go in by jeep, with a permit and a guide from the park office at Okanda. It is known for waterbirds, especially from about April to July. On a tour with us, the jeep, the permit and the park guide are included in your tour price.

### Is it worth visiting between November and March?

Only if you want somewhere very quiet. It is the north-east monsoon, with rain and onshore winds, and many guesthouses close. The south-west coast is in season then.

### Should I check travel advice before going to Arugam Bay?

Yes, as for any trip. Check your own government’s current travel advice before you travel.
