## The medieval capital

Polonnaruwa was a provincial town and military post for centuries before it became the capital. After Chola armies from South India took Anuradhapura in the late 10th century, they ruled from here. King Vijayabahu I drove them out and took the city in 1070, and for about two and a half centuries it was the seat of Sinhalese kings.

Its high point came under Parakramabahu I (1153–1186). He built the Parakrama Samudra, a reservoir of over 20 km² formed by joining older tanks, and much of the city you see today. A later king, Nissanka Malla (1187–1196), added shrines and inscriptions praising his own reign. After invasions and political turmoil from the 13th century, the capital was abandoned to the forest.

The ruins are more compact than Anuradhapura's. The builders worked mostly in brick, with Hindu shrines from the Chola period alongside the Buddhist monuments. UNESCO lists the Ancient City of Polonnaruwa as a World Heritage Site, and the Central Cultural Fund (CCF) has managed it since 1980.

## The ruins, from south to north

The main sites follow a road running north from the museum near the reservoir.

- **The royal palace.** Parakramabahu's palace was described as seven storeys high with a thousand rooms. Its massive brick walls still stand to about two storeys, with holes for the timber beams. Nearby are the king's audience hall and the Kumara Pokuna, a stepped royal bathing pool.
- **The Quadrangle.** This raised terrace held the city's most sacred buildings. The circular **Vatadage** has a carved moonstone and guardstones, and four seated Buddhas face its doorways. Opposite stands the **Hatadage**, Nissanka Malla's shrine for the Tooth Relic. Beside it lies the **Gal Pota**, a stone "book" of royal inscriptions. The **Nissanka Lata Mandapaya** has pillars carved like lotus stalks, and the **Thuparama** image house is a vaulted brick shrine.
- **Shiva Devale No. 2 and Rankoth Vehera.** The small stone Hindu temple dates from the Chola period. Rankoth Vehera, at about 54 m, is the city's largest stupa.
- **Alahana Pirivena.** This monastic university includes the **Lankatilaka**, whose tall brick walls frame a giant, now headless, Buddha image. Nearby, **Kiri Vehera** still has much of its original white lime plaster.
- **Gal Vihara.** Four Buddha images are cut from one granite face: a large seated figure, a smaller one in a carved cave, a standing figure of almost 7 m and a reclining Buddha of about 14 m. Some scholars think the standing figure is the disciple Ananda mourning the Buddha. This is still a place of worship, now under a protective shelter. Remove your shoes and hat, and don't pose with your back to the images.
- **Further north.** The **Lotus Pond** is a small stepped bath shaped like an open flower. The **Tivanka** image house holds most of the surviving wall paintings from the Polonnaruwa period.
- **South of the town.** By the reservoir, a large stone statue near **Potgul Vehera** is thought to show a king or a sage.

## Getting around the ruins

The museum near the reservoir is the starting point. Tickets are sold there, not at the ruins, and are checked at several sites, so keep yours to hand.

From the museum, the ruins stretch for several kilometres north along flat, tree-lined roads, with Gal Vihara about 5 km north of the town centre. Bicycles can be hired in town and near the site, which makes it easy to stop wherever you like. Others cover the distances by car or tuk-tuk and walk around each group.

## Practical advice

- **Start early.** The ticket office opens in the early morning, and the first hours are the coolest and quietest. Hours and prices are on the CCF ticket page, linked below.
- **Dress.** Cover your shoulders and knees, as several shrines are still sacred. You will take shoes off often, and sand and stone get very hot by midday.
- **Water.** Carry plenty. Shade is patchy, and the open ground around the monuments heats up quickly.
- **Monkeys.** Toque macaques live among the ruins. Keep food out of sight.

## Minneriya, Kaudulla and the tanks

Minneriya National Park is about 25–30 km west, on the road to Habarana, and Kaudulla lies to its north. In the dry season, roughly July to October, elephants come to graze around the shrinking reservoirs, and large numbers gather at Minneriya around August and September. Herds move between the parks, and sightings are never guaranteed. The Giritale tank, built in the 7th century, lies on the same road.

## How long to stay

**A full morning** covers the main groups at a steady pace. **One night** lets you start at opening time and add a safari or a slower second look. A typical day:

- **Early morning:** the museum for tickets, then the royal palace and the Quadrangle before the heat.
- **Mid-morning:** Shiva Devale, Rankoth Vehera, Alahana Pirivena and Gal Vihara.
- **Late morning:** the Lotus Pond and Tivanka if you have energy, then lunch.
- **Afternoon:** a jeep safari in Minneriya or Kaudulla, or the drive on.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport.** Polonnaruwa is roughly 220 km away by road, usually 5–6 hours. Most routes reach it from [Sigiriya](/places/sigiriya/), about 60 km and 1.5 hours to the west, either as a day trip or for a night.
- **By rail.** Trains from Colombo Fort usually take about 5–6 hours. Services were disrupted after Cyclone Ditwah in late 2025, so check the current timetable with Sri Lanka Railways.
- **Onward.** [Trincomalee](/places/trincomalee/) is about 105 km north-east, around 2–2.5 hours. [Dambulla](/places/dambulla/) is about 1.5 hours west, [Anuradhapura](/places/anuradhapura/) about 2–2.5 hours north-west, and [Kandy](/places/kandy/) about 3.5–4 hours.

In a private tour you add Polonnaruwa to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Best time to visit

Polonnaruwa is in the dry zone. Most rain falls from about October to January, usually as heavy afternoon showers. The rest of the year is mostly dry and hot, especially around April and May. The ruins can be visited in any month, but the heat makes an early start important.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I need in Polonnaruwa?

A full morning covers the main sites. A whole day, or a night nearby, lets you see the outlying ruins too and add an elephant safari.

### Can I cycle around Polonnaruwa?

Yes. The roads between the ruins are flat and mostly shaded, and bicycles can be hired in town. Start early and take water.

### Where do I buy the ticket?

At the ticket office by the Archaeological Museum, near the reservoir, before you go to the ruins. Current prices and hours are on the Central Cultural Fund's ticket page.

### Is Polonnaruwa worth visiting if I have seen Anuradhapura?

Yes. Anuradhapura is a pilgrimage city of great stupas; Polonnaruwa is a compact medieval capital with a royal palace and the Gal Vihara carvings. It is also easier to see in a morning.

### Can I combine Polonnaruwa with an elephant safari?

Yes. Minneriya is about 25–30 km away. Many visitors see the ruins in the morning and take a jeep safari in the afternoon, especially from July to October.
