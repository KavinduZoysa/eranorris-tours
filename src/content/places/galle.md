## Why visit Galle

Galle stands on a rocky headland on the south-west coast, about 120 km south of Colombo. It was a trading port long before Europeans arrived, and it remained the island’s main harbour until Colombo’s new harbour took over in the late 19th century. The Portuguese first fortified the headland in the late 16th century. The Dutch East India Company took the town in 1640 and spent the next century rebuilding its defences in stone. The British took over in 1796.

UNESCO inscribed the Old Town of Galle and its Fortifications in 1988, citing the way European planning meets South Asian building traditions. What makes a visit rewarding is that it is not a museum piece. The 52 hectares inside the walls are still homes, schools, shops, mosques and churches, with guesthouses and cafés in between.

## Walking the ramparts

The walls run right round the headland, linking fourteen bastions, and walking along the top is the easiest way to understand the place. From the Clock Tower by the Main Gate, the walls lead west and then south beside the open sea to Flag Rock, the old signal point at the southern tip. The lighthouse stands just beyond it on Point Utrecht Bastion; the present tower dates from 1939, replacing one lost to fire. From there the walls turn north along the harbour side towards the Old Gate.

A full circuit is roughly 2–3 km on uneven ground with little shade, so go early in the morning or in the last couple of hours before sunset, when residents come out to walk and watch the light go from the western walls. Some stretches have unguarded edges, so keep an eye on children. Walking the walls and streets costs nothing.

## Inside the walls

The streets keep their Dutch grid, lined with low houses fronted by shady verandas. A few places reward a closer look:

- **The Dutch Reformed Church**, completed in 1755, is paved with gravestones moved from the old Dutch cemeteries. All Saints’ Church (1871) and the Meeran Mosque near the lighthouse (1904) came later. Dress modestly if you go in.
- **The Old Gate** on the harbour side carries the Dutch company’s monogram and the date 1669. Above it, the Maritime Archaeology Museum fills a 17th-century warehouse with finds from Dutch shipwrecks. It was rebuilt after the 2004 tsunami.
- **The National Museum of Galle** occupies a single-storey building from 1656, thought to be the oldest Dutch building in the fort.
- **The Old Dutch Hospital**, a long colonnaded building near the lighthouse, has been restored and now houses shops and restaurants.

The Galle Heritage Foundation runs an information centre and small galleries. Opening times and fees for all of these change, so check the official pages below. This is a residential town: ask before photographing people or their homes. Flying a drone needs permission from the Civil Aviation Authority.

## Unawatuna, Jungle Beach and Rumassala

The fort has no real swimming beach. For the sea, most visitors head east to Unawatuna, about 5–6 km away. It is a crescent bay sheltered by headlands and a reef, and usually calm enough to swim from about November to April.

Between Galle harbour and Unawatuna rises the wooded Rumassala headland, linked in local legend to a fragment of the Himalayas dropped by Hanuman in the Ramayana. Jungle Beach is a small cove below it. On the ridge above, a white peace pagoda built by Japanese Buddhist monks looks back across the bay to the fort.

Further east, at Koggala and Ahangama, you will see stilt fishermen perched on poles in the surf. These days most of them pose for photographs for a fee rather than fish.

## How long to stay

**Half a day** is enough to walk the ramparts and a few streets. **A full day** adds a museum, lunch in the fort and an afternoon at Unawatuna. **One or two nights** let you see the walls at dawn and dusk, when the day visitors have gone, and give you a slower beach day.

A typical full day:

- **Early morning:** the ramparts from the Clock Tower to the lighthouse, before the heat.
- **Late morning:** the churches, the Old Gate and a museum.
- **Afternoon:** Unawatuna or Jungle Beach for a swim in season.
- **Evening:** back on the western walls for sunset.

## Best time to visit

Galle lies on the wet south-west coast. It tends to be driest from about December to April. The south-west monsoon, from about May to September, brings showers and a rough sea, and the October–November inter-monsoon often brings afternoon storms. The fort is worth visiting in any month; it is the beaches that depend on the season. During the monsoon the Coast Guard warns of strong currents on southern beaches, so swim only where it is clearly safe and lifeguards are present.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport.** About 150 km, usually 2–2.5 hours by the expressways, longer if traffic around Colombo is heavy.
- **From Colombo.** About 1.5–2 hours by road. The coastal railway takes roughly 2–3 hours, and Galle station is a short walk from the Main Gate.
- **Along the coast.** [Hikkaduwa](/places/hikkaduwa/) is about 30–45 minutes north and [Bentota](/places/bentota/) about 1–1.5 hours. [Mirissa](/places/mirissa/) is about 45 minutes to an hour east.
- **Inland and east.** [Udawalawe](/places/udawalawe/) is about 2.5–3 hours and [Yala](/places/yala/) about 3–3.5 hours, mostly on the Southern Expressway.
- **In the fort.** Cars can enter, but the lanes are narrow and everything is within walking distance.

In a private tour you add Galle to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a ticket to enter Galle Fort?

No. The fort is a working town, and the ramparts and streets are open to walk at any time. The museums and some galleries charge for entry; check their official pages for current fees and hours.

### Is it worth staying inside the fort?

If you enjoy quiet evenings and early walks, yes. The fort is busiest from mid-morning to late afternoon, and a night inside lets you walk the walls when it is cool and calm. If you prefer the beach, Unawatuna is a short drive away.

### When is the best time of day to walk the ramparts?

Early morning or the last two hours before sunset. There is little shade on the walls, and the western side is where people gather to watch the sun go down.

### Can I swim at Galle?

Not really in the fort itself. Unawatuna and Jungle Beach, a few kilometres east, are the usual places to swim, mainly from about November to April. From May to September the sea on this coast can be rough, with strong currents.

### Is Galle a good base for whale watching at Mirissa?

It can be, but boats leave Mirissa early, often around 6–7 am, and the drive takes about 45 minutes to an hour. Many people stay in [Mirissa](/places/mirissa/) the night before a trip instead.
