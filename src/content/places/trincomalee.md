## A harbour town on the north-east coast

Trincomalee, usually shortened to Trinco, sits on a peninsula beside a large, deep natural harbour that ships can use in any weather. As Gokanna it was a trading port in the age of the ancient kingdoms. Later the Portuguese, Dutch, French and British fought over it; the British held it from 1795 until 1948, and Japanese aircraft attacked the harbour in April 1942.

The town is a working place rather than a resort, with Tamil, Muslim and Sinhalese communities, Hindu temples, fish markets and naval bases. The east coast was badly affected by the civil war and by the 2004 tsunami, and tourism here only grew again after the war ended in 2009. Most visitors stay on the beaches north of town and come in for the sights.

## Fort Frederick, Swami Rock and Koneswaram

The headland east of the centre is the heart of old Trincomalee. In 1622 the Portuguese destroyed the great Koneswaram temple on Swami Rock and built a fort with its stones. The Dutch took it in 1639 and rebuilt it, and the British held it from 1795. It is still an army garrison, but visitors pass through the gate on the way to the temple, and spotted deer often graze on its lawns.

Koneswaram, rebuilt in the mid-20th century on the cliff top, is one of Sri Lanka’s five ancient shrines to Shiva and a busy place of worship, especially at puja times and festivals such as the chariot festival, Navaratri and Sivarathri. Remove your shoes before entering and cover shoulders and knees. The rock drops sheer to the sea, and in season whales are sometimes spotted from here.

## Uppuveli, Nilaveli and Marble Beach

- **Uppuveli**, a few kilometres north of town, is a fishing village with guesthouses and small hotels along a long, sandy beach. It is the most convenient base, with the most places to eat.
- **Nilaveli**, about 15 km north of town, is quieter, with a long, gently shelving beach. Boats for Pigeon Island leave from here.
- **Marble Beach**, south of the harbour near China Bay, is a sheltered cove run by the Sri Lanka Air Force, with a public area.

In season the sea is often flat, warm and shallow close to shore. Don’t assume a lifeguard is on duty: swim where local people swim and take care near rocks and river mouths.

## Pigeon Island National Park

About 1 km off Nilaveli, Pigeon Island National Park protects two small islands and the coral around them. Sri Lanka Tourism describes its reefs as among the best remaining in the country, and blacktip reef sharks and turtles are often seen in the shallows. It became a sanctuary in 1963 and a national park in 2003; rock pigeons breed on the islands and give the park its name.

Most visitors snorkel off the beach of the larger island. Crowding, anchoring and bleaching have damaged parts of the reef, and some areas are roped off to let it recover.

- **Don’t stand on coral or feed the fish**, and take all your rubbish back with you.
- **Fees, opening hours and rules** are set by the Department of Wildlife Conservation and change, so check them before you go.
- **Boats can stop in rough weather**, mainly during the north-east monsoon.

## Whale and dolphin watching

Blue whales, smaller numbers of sperm whales and schools of spinner dolphins are seen off this coast. The season off Trincomalee runs from roughly March or April to August or September, mostly outside the November–April season around [Mirissa](/places/mirissa/) on the south coast. Whales are usually found about 6–8 nautical miles offshore, around half an hour by boat. Trips leave early in the morning, and sightings are never guaranteed. Choose an operator that keeps to the national rules on approaching whales and dolphins.

## More to see around Trincomalee

- **Trincomalee War Cemetery**, on the road north to Nilaveli, holds the graves of more than 300 Commonwealth service personnel from the Second World War, when Trincomalee was an important naval base.
- **Kanniya hot wells**, about 8 km west of town, are seven small square wells of warm water. Hindu tradition links them to King Ravana, and the remains of an early Buddhist monastery lie beside them. People bathe by pouring water from buckets, so wear modest clothing rather than swimwear alone.

## When to go

Trincomalee has the opposite season to the south-west coast. It is driest, and the sea calmest, from about May to September, which suits travellers in the northern summer when Galle and Hikkaduwa are in their monsoon. The wet season runs from about October to January, wettest around November and December. Then the sea is rough, Pigeon Island boats may not run and some beach hotels close.

## How long to stay

**Two nights** allow a morning on Pigeon Island, an afternoon at Fort Frederick and Koneswaram, and time on the beach. A **third night** leaves room for a whale-watching trip in season, or simply a slower pace.

A typical day:

- **Morning:** a boat from Nilaveli to Pigeon Island to snorkel while the water is calm.
- **Midday:** lunch and shade back on the beach.
- **Afternoon:** Fort Frederick and Koneswaram as the heat eases.
- **Evening:** dinner on the beach at Uppuveli.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport or Colombo.** Around 5–6 hours by road, longer with stops. The train from Colombo Fort takes roughly 7–8 hours, with a day train and an overnight service. Services were disrupted after Cyclone Ditwah in late 2025, so check Sri Lanka Railways for the current timetable.
- **From the Cultural Triangle.** [Sigiriya](/places/sigiriya/) and Habarana are about 2–2.5 hours away, [Polonnaruwa](/places/polonnaruwa/) about 2–2.5 hours and [Anuradhapura](/places/anuradhapura/) about 2.5–3 hours. Trincomalee makes an easy beach break after the ancient cities.
- **From Kandy.** About 4–5 hours by road.
- **Down the east coast.** [Arugam Bay](/places/arugam-bay/) is about 5 hours or more south, via Batticaloa.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time to visit Trincomalee?

From about May to September, when the east coast is dry and the sea calm. From about October to January rain is heavy and the sea can be rough.

### How do I get to Pigeon Island?

By boat from Nilaveli beach; the islands are about 1 km offshore. The national park fee and the boat are paid separately, and boats may not run in rough weather. Check current fees and hours before you go.

### Can I see whales from Trincomalee?

Often, in season. Blue and sperm whales are seen offshore from about March to August, and spinner dolphins are common. Trips last a few hours, and sightings are never guaranteed.

### Should I stay in Uppuveli or Nilaveli?

Uppuveli is closer to town, with more places to eat and stay. Nilaveli is quieter, with a longer beach and the boats to Pigeon Island.

### What should I wear at Koneswaram?

Cover your shoulders and knees, and remove your shoes before entering. It is an active Hindu temple, so keep your voice down during pujas.
