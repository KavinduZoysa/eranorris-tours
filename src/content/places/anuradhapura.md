## The sacred city

Anuradhapura became the royal capital in the 4th century BC and remained so for well over a thousand years, until invasions from the Chola kingdom of South India in the late 10th and early 11th centuries pushed the court to Polonnaruwa. Buddhism reached the island in the 3rd century BC at nearby Mihintale, and the city grew around three great monasteries, the Mahavihara, Abhayagiri and Jetavana, which were rival schools of Theravada Buddhism. Its kings also built reservoirs, or tanks, that still water the farmland.

UNESCO lists the Sacred City of Anuradhapura as a World Heritage Site, but it is not only a ruin. The great stupas and the Bodhi tree are places of daily worship, busy with pilgrims dressed in white. The modern New Town lies to the east of the sacred area.

## The Bodhi tree and the old monastery

The southern part of the sacred city grew around the Mahavihara, the oldest of the monasteries.

- **Sri Maha Bodhi.** This sacred fig grew from a branch of the tree at Bodh Gaya under which the Buddha is believed to have attained enlightenment. The nun Sanghamitta brought it in the 3rd century BC, and it is considered the oldest living planted tree with a documented history. It grows on a raised terrace behind golden railings, and pilgrims make offerings on the terraces below. Expect a security check.
- **Ruwanwelisaya.** King Dutugemunu began this great white stupa in the 2nd century BC. Restored in the 20th century, it now stands about 100 m high, ringed by a wall of carved elephants.
- **Thuparamaya.** Said to be the island's first stupa, it enshrines a collarbone relic of the Buddha and is ringed by slender stone pillars.
- **Lovamahapaya.** A fenced field of about 1,600 stone columns is all that remains of the Brazen Palace, a many-storeyed monastic hall named after its bronze roof tiles.

## Abhayagiri and Jetavana

North of the centre, the **Abhayagiri** monastery spreads across a large wooded area. King Valagamba founded it in the 1st century BC, and the Chinese monk Faxian described it after his visit in the 5th century. Its huge brick stupa stands among ruins that include the **Samadhi Buddha**, a seated stone figure in meditation; the **Kuttam Pokuna**, a pair of stone-lined bathing ponds; and, at the building known as Mahasena's Palace, the best-known of the island's moonstones, the carved half-moon stones at the foot of entrance steps.

To the east, King Mahasena began the **Jetavanaramaya** in the 3rd century AD. At about 122 m it was one of the tallest structures of the ancient world, built from roughly 93 million bricks. It stands about 71 m high today. Site museums at Abhayagiri and Jetavana display finds from the excavations.

## Isurumuniya and the tanks

**Isurumuniya** is a small rock temple beside the Tissa Wewa reservoir. Its carvings include elephants bathing in a pool and a couple known as the Isurumuniya Lovers. It charges its own entry fee. The banks of the old tanks make a pleasant walk in the cool of the evening.

## Visiting the sacred city

- **Tickets.** A Central Cultural Fund ticket covers the archaeological areas and museums, and staff check it at several points. The CCF ticket page, linked below, has current hours and prices.
- **Getting around.** The sites spread over several kilometres. Move between them by car or tuk-tuk, or cycle the flat roads. Allow a full day.
- **Dress and conduct.** Cover your shoulders and knees. Remove shoes and hats at stupas, temples and the Bodhi tree. Walk clockwise around stupas, and don't pose with your back to a Buddha image. Many Sri Lankans wear white to worship, and visitors are welcome to do the same.
- **Heat.** You walk barefoot on stone and sand, which become very hot by midday. Visit early and late, and carry light socks.
- **Full-moon days.** Each month's poya day brings crowds of pilgrims, and many temples stay open into the night: memorable for worship, slow for sightseeing.

## Mihintale and other side trips

- **Mihintale.** About 12 km east, this hill is where tradition says the monk Mahinda met King Devanampiya Tissa and brought Buddhism to Sri Lanka. A stairway of 1,840 granite steps climbs past the ruins of a monastery hospital to stupas and the rock of Aradhana Gala. Climb early or late in the day. It is busiest at the Poson full moon in June.
- **Aukana.** A 5th-century standing Buddha nearly 12 m tall, off the road south to [Dambulla](/places/dambulla/).
- **Ritigala.** Forest monastery ruins about 40 km south-east, on a mountain that is otherwise a closed nature reserve.
- **Wilpattu National Park.** The park's main gate is about 30 km west. Dense forest surrounds natural lakes, and leopards, sloth bears and elephants live here. Visits are by jeep, and sightings are never guaranteed.

## How long to stay

**One night** gives you a late afternoon and a full day. **Two nights** add Mihintale at an easy pace, or Wilpattu. A typical visit:

- **Arrival afternoon:** the Sri Maha Bodhi and Ruwanwelisaya during evening worship.
- **Next morning, early:** Abhayagiri, the Samadhi Buddha and the twin ponds, then Jetavanaramaya.
- **Midday:** a museum, or a rest out of the sun.
- **Late afternoon:** Isurumuniya, then Mihintale's steps as the heat fades, or the drive on.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport.** Anuradhapura is roughly 190–200 km by road, usually 4–5 hours, depending on traffic.
- **By rail.** Trains from Colombo Fort usually take about 4.5–5 hours. The Northern Line was closed for several months after Cyclone Ditwah in late 2025, so check the current timetable with Sri Lanka Railways.
- **Onward.** [Sigiriya](/places/sigiriya/) is around 2 hours south-east. Dambulla is 1.5–2 hours south, and [Polonnaruwa](/places/polonnaruwa/) about 100 km away, 2–2.5 hours. [Trincomalee](/places/trincomalee/) is about 107 km east, 2–2.5 hours via Mihintale.

In a private tour you add Anuradhapura to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Best time to visit

Anuradhapura is in the dry zone and warm all year. Most rain falls from about October to January; April and May are usually the hottest months. The religious calendar matters as much as the weather. The Vesak full moon, usually in May, and Poson in June draw large crowds of pilgrims, and hotels fill early.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I need in Anuradhapura?

One full day covers the Bodhi tree, the great stupas and the main monastery ruins. Stay a second night to add Mihintale or a Wilpattu safari without rushing.

### What should I wear?

Clothes that cover your shoulders and knees, and shoes you can slip off easily, as you remove them at every sacred site. White clothing is customary for worship but not required.

### Is Mihintale worth the climb?

Yes, if you are interested in how Buddhism came to Sri Lanka. There are 1,840 steps, which you can take at your own pace, and the summit looks out over the plains. Go early or late, and expect large crowds at Poson in June.

### Can I visit Anuradhapura as a day trip from Sigiriya?

It is possible but long, at around 2 hours each way. One night in Anuradhapura lets you see the Bodhi tree at evening worship and the ruins in the cool of the morning.
