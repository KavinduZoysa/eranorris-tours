## Where the river meets the sea

Bentota lies about 65 km south of Colombo, at the mouth of the Bentota River, also called the Bentara Ganga. Aluthgama is on the north bank and Bentota on the south. From the river mouth a long beach runs south towards Induruwa, with the railway and the Galle Road just behind it.

Travellers stopped here long before tourism. The Portuguese built a small fort at the river mouth in the 17th century, and under the Dutch and British the rest house was the halfway halt between Colombo and Galle. In the late 1960s Bentota was chosen as Sri Lanka’s first planned national holiday resort, and Geoffrey Bawa’s Bentota Beach Hotel, built on the old rest-house site, set a style that many later Sri Lankan hotels followed.

That history explains its character today: resort hotels, villas and Ayurveda spas, set well apart. It is quieter and more spread out than [Hikkaduwa](/places/hikkaduwa/), with fewer bars and cafés along the beach.

## The beach and the sea

The beach is long, wide and lined with palms, and it faces west for the sunset. Like the rest of this coast, it is at its best from about November to April, when the north-east monsoon brings sunshine and calmer seas. From about May to October the south-west monsoon brings rain and heavy surf; swimming is often restricted and red flags are common.

- **Currents can be strong in any month**, especially near the river mouth, where the river meets the sea.
- **Swim near hotel lifeguards** where there are any, and follow their flags.
- **Keep out of the sea in the monsoon** when the flags are up, however inviting it looks.

## On the river

The river is the other half of Bentota. Upstream from the bridges it winds between mangroves, small islands and coconut land, and its sheltered water is used for boat trips and water sports.

**River safaris** usually last one to three hours. Kingfishers, herons and cormorants are common, water monitors are often seen and crocodiles sometimes, though nothing is guaranteed. Mornings and late afternoons are cooler and better for birds. Watch animals from the boat; don’t handle or feed them.

**Water sports** such as jet skis, water-skiing, wakeboarding, banana boats and windsurfing mostly take place on the sheltered river and lagoon near the mouth rather than in the open sea. Operators vary, so check that life jackets are provided and fit.

## Lunuganga and Brief: the Bawa gardens

Two gardens inland make Bentota a good stop for anyone interested in architecture and landscape.

- **Lunuganga** was the country estate of the architect Geoffrey Bawa. He bought the former rubber and cinnamon plantation in 1948 and spent decades shaping it into a garden of terraces, lake views and pavilions. The Geoffrey Bawa Trust runs it and leads guided tours at set times each day; tours by outside guides are not permitted. Expect steep steps and uneven paths.
- **Brief Garden**, about 10 km inland near Kalawila, belonged to his brother, the landscape designer Bevis Bawa. It is a looser, more personal garden on a former rubber estate, full of sculpture and long open to visitors.

## More to see nearby

- **Kosgoda**, about 11 km south, has several privately run turtle hatcheries. Eggs are incubated there and hatchlings kept in tanks before release; they are captive facilities, not wild encounters.
- **Galapata Raja Maha Vihara**, inland from Bentota, is an old temple with medieval stone inscriptions, carved pillars and ponds.
- **Madu Ganga** at Balapitiya, about 20 km south, is a mangrove estuary with islands and an island temple reached by boat. It is a Ramsar wetland of international importance.
- **Ambalangoda**, a little further south, is known for its carved wooden masks.

## How long to stay

**Two nights** give you a morning on the river, an afternoon at one of the gardens and time on the beach. Many travellers stay longer at the end of a tour: Bentota is one of the easier beaches to reach from the airport, so a final rest here leaves a manageable transfer home.

A typical day:

- **Early morning:** a river safari, while the water is still and birds are active.
- **Late morning:** the beach, or water sports on the river.
- **Afternoon:** a guided tour of Lunuganga, or Brief Garden.
- **Evening:** sunset on the beach.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport.** About 1.5–2.5 hours by road, via the expressways and the Southern Expressway’s Welipenna exit, or slower along the Galle Road. Traffic around Colombo is the main variable.
- **By train.** Most coastal-line trains stop at Aluthgama, just north of the river, rather than at the small Bentota station. From Colombo Fort the journey takes roughly 1–1.5 hours.
- **South along the coast.** Hikkaduwa is about 40 km south, [Galle](/places/galle/) about 60 km, and [Mirissa](/places/mirissa/) around 2 hours away.
- **From the hills.** [Kandy](/places/kandy/) is about 3–4 hours by road.

In a private tour you add Bentota to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you swim at Bentota all year?

Not safely. From about November to April the sea is usually calm enough. From May to October the south-west monsoon brings heavy surf and strong currents, and swimming is often restricted. Follow lifeguards’ flags in any month.

### What is the Bentota river safari like?

A boat trip of one to three hours along the river and its mangrove channels, looking for birds, water monitors and sometimes crocodiles. Go early or late in the day, and expect no guarantees.

### Can I visit Lunuganga without booking?

The Geoffrey Bawa Trust runs garden tours at set times each day and says the standard tour needs no reservation; private and curatorial tours must be booked ahead. Outside guides cannot lead tours. Check times and fees on the Trust’s website.

### Bentota or Hikkaduwa?

Bentota is quieter and more resort-based, with the river for boat trips and water sports. Hikkaduwa is livelier, with a reef for snorkelling and turtles close to shore. They are about an hour apart, so it is easy to see both.

### Are the Kosgoda turtle hatcheries a wild turtle experience?

No. They are privately run facilities where turtles are kept in tanks and eggs are incubated before release. To look for wild turtles, try the shallows at Hikkaduwa, where sightings are common but never guaranteed.
