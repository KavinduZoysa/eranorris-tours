## The town and the beach

Hikkaduwa lies about 100 km south of Colombo and 20 km north of Galle, strung out for several kilometres between the Galle Road, the coastal railway and the sea. It has drawn travellers since the 1960s, and the main stretch is a busy run of guesthouses, dive shops, cafés and bars, with music late into the evening in season.

The northern end, by the reef, is the busiest and in places badly eroded, with little dry sand at high tide. Further south, Wewala and Narigama have wider sand, and Thiranagama at the far end is quieter still.

## The reef and the marine national park

Just off the northern beach lies Hikkaduwa National Park, a shallow fringing reef of about 100 hectares, on average only around 5 m deep. It became Sri Lanka’s first marine sanctuary in 1979 and a national park in 2002, and it is managed by the Department of Wildlife Conservation.

Bleaching in 1998 killed much of the live coral, and boats, trampling and pollution have added to the damage. Restoration began in 2023, and the ban on touching or damaging coral is now enforced: in 2025 an offender was prosecuted and fined. Reef fish are still plentiful, and turtles are seen regularly.

- **Snorkel from the beach on calm days**, keeping to the marked swimming and snorkelling areas.
- **Never stand on or touch coral.** At low tide the water over the reef is very shallow, so keep your fins up.
- **Glass-bottom boats** show the reef without getting wet, though boats hitting the shallow reef top have been one cause of damage.
- **Divers** can join dry-season trips to deeper reefs and wrecks such as the *Conch*, a tanker lost in 1903.

## Sea turtles, wild and captive

Green turtles come into the shallows by the reef to graze on algae, often a few metres from swimmers; the stretch at the north end is known as Turtle Beach. Sightings are common but depend on the sea and are never guaranteed. All sea turtles are protected in Sri Lanka. Give them space, don’t touch or chase them, and don’t feed them, even with the seaweed sold on the beach. Feeding changes their natural behaviour.

The turtle hatcheries along this coast are a different thing. They are privately run facilities where eggs taken from nesting beaches are incubated before the hatchlings are released, and where turtles are kept in tanks. A visit shows you captive turtles, not a wild encounter.

## Sea conditions and swimming safety

Hikkaduwa’s season is the north-east monsoon, roughly November to April, when Sri Lanka Tourism describes this coast as sunny with calm seas. From about May to October the south-west monsoon brings rain, heavy swell and stronger currents, and red flags go up more often.

Even in season, currents can build quickly; the Coast Guard lifesavers on the main beach make rescues in calm months too. Swim where others swim and near the lifesavers, obey flags and warning signs, and stay out of the water at night or after drinking.

**Surfing** follows the same season. Reef breaks off the main beach suit experienced surfers; the gentler waves further south, around Narigama, are where most lessons and board hire happen. Reef and rock lie close to the surface at some breaks, so ask locally before paddling out.

## The 2004 tsunami

The Boxing Day tsunami hit this coast hard. At Peraliya, a few kilometres north of the town, the wave struck a crowded Colombo–Matara train, the *Queen of the Sea*. Estimates vary, but well over a thousand people are thought to have died, making it the worst rail disaster on record.

A memorial stands beside the line at Peraliya, and a 30 m Buddha statue, given by Japanese donors, honours the victims. Small community museums along the Galle Road at Peraliya and Telwatta tell the story through photographs, press cuttings and personal accounts; some images are graphic. They are places of remembrance for local families.

## Around Hikkaduwa

- **Ambalangoda**, about 15 km north, is known for carved wooden masks, from the *sanni* masks of healing rituals to the *kolam* masks of folk theatre, with family workshops and museums.
- **Madu Ganga** at Balapitiya, a little further north, is a mangrove estuary scattered with islands and a Ramsar wetland of international importance. Boat trips usually stop at the temple on Kothduwa island.
- **Rathgama Lagoon**, just south of town, is calm and fringed with mangroves.
- **Moonstone mines** at Meetiyagoda, inland towards Ambalangoda, are small hand-dug pits where you can see the stones washed and cut.
- **[Galle](/places/galle/)**, about 20 km south, has its walled fort, with the bay at Unawatuna just beyond.

## How long to stay

**One night** is enough to snorkel, look for turtles and catch a sunset. **Two or three nights** let you add Galle, the Madu River or Ambalangoda, and leave a spare morning if the sea is rough. Surfers and divers often stay much longer.

A typical day:

- **Early morning:** snorkel or look for turtles while the sea is calm and the beach empty.
- **Late morning:** a boat on the Madu River, or the masks at Ambalangoda.
- **Afternoon:** the tsunami memorial and museums at Peraliya, then a rest through the heat.
- **Evening:** sunset on the beach and dinner along the strip.

## Getting there and moving on

- **From the airport.** About 2–2.5 hours by road via the expressways around Colombo and the Southern Expressway, longer in heavy traffic.
- **By train.** Hikkaduwa is on the coastal line from Colombo Fort to Matara, which runs beside the sea for much of the way.
- **Along the coast.** Galle is about 30–45 minutes south, [Mirissa](/places/mirissa/) about 1–1.5 hours, and [Bentota](/places/bentota/) about 45 minutes–1 hour north.

In a private tour you add Hikkaduwa to your route on the map, and your driver handles the roads. The drive times shown in the planner are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to swim at Hikkaduwa?

Usually, from about November to April, when the sea is calmer. From May to October it is often rough, and currents can form in any month. Swim near the lifesavers, follow the flags and signs, and stay out when the locals do.

### Will I see sea turtles?

Often, but never guaranteed. Green turtles graze in the shallows by the reef at the north end of the beach, mostly when the sea is calm. Watch from a distance, and don’t touch or feed them.

### Is a turtle hatchery the same as seeing wild turtles?

No. Hatcheries are privately run facilities where turtles are kept in tanks and eggs are incubated before release. Turtles in the sea off the beach are wild.

### Is the coral still worth seeing?

Bleaching and heavy use have damaged much of the reef, and restoration is under way. You will still see reef fish, and often turtles, in shallow water. Touching or standing on coral is prohibited.

### When is the surf season?

Roughly November to April, the dry season on this coast. There are reef breaks for experienced surfers and softer waves further south for beginners.
