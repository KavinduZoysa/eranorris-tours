## A mountain sacred to four faiths

Sri Pada means “sacred footprint”. Near the summit is a footprint-shaped hollow in the rock, about 1.8 m long, which Buddhists venerate as the Buddha’s footprint, many Hindus as Shiva’s, and some Muslims and Christians as Adam’s or St Thomas’s. The mountain is also called Samanalakanda, and Buddhist tradition holds that the god Saman watches over those who climb it. Pilgrims have made the ascent for more than a thousand years; the Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta climbed it in 1344.

The cone rises steeply from the forests of the Peak Wilderness Sanctuary, protected since 1940 and one of the three parts of UNESCO’s Central Highlands of Sri Lanka World Heritage site. The Kelani, Kalu and Walawe rivers rise on its slopes.

## The pilgrimage season

The season opens on Unduvap Poya, the full moon of December, when sacred objects and the image of the god Saman are taken in procession to the summit, and it closes on Vesak Poya in May. In these months the path is lit all night, tea stalls and rest halls stay open along the way, and the weather is at its most settled. January to March usually brings the clearest dawns.

The busiest climbs are on poya (full moon) days, weekends and public holidays, when queues form on the final steps. April is the peak of the pilgrimage. Climb on a weekday if you can.

Outside the season the lights are off, the stalls are closed, and the south-west monsoon brings heavy rain, wind and mist. The steps are slippery, leeches are common, and landslides can close sections of the path. An off-season climb is only for experienced walkers with up-to-date local advice.

## The climb from Dalhousie

Most visitors climb from Dalhousie, also called Nallathanni, about 32 km beyond Hatton. This route is the shortest and steepest, and it has the most facilities. The trail starts at about 1,250 m, so you gain roughly 1,000 m over about 5–7 km, almost all of it on steps. Common estimates put the number at around 5,000–5,500.

- **Timing.** Fit walkers take about 3–4 hours up and 2–3 hours down, longer on busy nights. Sunrise is around 6–6.30 am, so most people set off between about 1.30 and 2.30 am.
- **On the way.** The path leaves the village through tea estates and reaches the Japanese Peace Pagoda, roughly halfway. Higher up, pilgrims bathe at the Seetha Gangula stream. At Indikatu Pahana they throw a threaded needle into the bushes, recalling a legend of the Buddha mending his robe. The last section, Mahagiridamba, is the steepest, with handrails on both sides.
- **At the top.** On a clear morning the mountain casts a sharp triangular shadow across the clouds and hills to the west for a short time after sunrise. Cloud often hides it. The summit is cold and windy while you wait, so time your start rather than racing up.
- **The longer route.** The Ratnapura–Palabaddala route on the south-west side is far longer, about 15 km and around seven hours up, and is used by many Sri Lankan pilgrims.

## Etiquette on a pilgrim path

You will climb alongside families, elderly pilgrims and monks for whom this is an act of devotion.

- Pilgrims greet each other with “karunavai”, and asking how far is left is considered disrespectful.
- Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, and take off your shoes and hat at the summit terrace as others do.
- Give way to slower and elderly pilgrims on narrow sections, and keep your voice low at the summit.
- The summit bell is rung once for each climb a pilgrim has made.
- Ask before photographing people at prayer.

## What to bring

The climb is warm work, but the summit is cold and often windy before dawn.

- Warm layers, a hat and a rain jacket.
- A head torch for unlit stretches.
- Water in a refillable bottle and snacks in a reusable box.
- Small notes for tea and food at the stalls.
- Shoes with grip. The descent is hard on knees, and walking poles help.

## Keep the mountain clean

Litter has long been a problem on the pilgrim trails, and the IUCN, which advises UNESCO, names waste and visitor pressure among the main threats to the site. For the 2025–26 season the government approved rules for the Sri Pada area banning single-use plastic bottles under one litre, polythene wrappers and bags, and sachets. Carry all your rubbish back down.

## How long to stay

One night at the foot of the mountain is the usual plan. Some people sleep for a few hours after the climb and drive on; others stay a second night, as legs can be stiff for a day or two.

A typical climb:

- **Afternoon:** arrive in Dalhousie, eat early and go to bed.
- **1.30–2.30 am:** start the climb.
- **Around 6–6.30 am:** sunrise at the summit.
- **Mid-morning:** back in Dalhousie for breakfast and a rest.

## Getting there and moving on

Hatton is the usual gateway. From there the road runs through Maskeliya and past the Maussakelle reservoir to Dalhousie, about 1–1.5 hours.

- **From Colombo.** About 4–5 hours by road via Avissawella and then Hatton or Norton Bridge. From the airport, allow longer.
- **From Kandy.** About 3–4.5 hours by road.
- **To Nuwara Eliya.** About 2.5–3.5 hours to [Nuwara Eliya](/places/nuwara-eliya/) via Talawakele, where St Clair’s and Devon falls have roadside viewpoints, and on from there to [Ella](/places/ella/).
- **By train.** Hatton is on the hill-country line, but after Cyclone Ditwah several sections were still closed for repair at the time of writing. Check Sri Lanka Railways before relying on it.

In a private tour you add Adam’s Peak to your route on the map, usually between Colombo or [Kandy](/places/kandy/) and the tea country, and your driver handles the roads. The planner’s drive times are rough estimates, not road-tested figures.

## Frequently asked questions

### How hard is the climb?

It is demanding but not technical: several thousand steps and about 1,000 m of ascent, done at night. Anyone reasonably fit who takes regular breaks can manage it.

### When does the pilgrimage season start and end?

It runs from Unduvap Poya, the full moon in December, to Vesak Poya in May. The dates follow the lunar calendar and change every year.

### Can I climb Adam’s Peak outside the season?

It is possible, but the path is unlit, the stalls are closed, and rain, mist, leeches and landslides are all risks. Most visitors should plan to climb between December and May.

### What time should I start climbing?

From Dalhousie, most people start between about 1.30 and 2.30 am to reach the summit for sunrise. Start earlier on poya days and weekends, when queues build on the final steps.

### Will I see the shadow of the peak?

On a clear morning, yes, for a short time after sunrise. Cloud often hides it, so treat it as a bonus.

### Do I need a guide?

In season the path is lit, busy and easy to follow, and most people climb without one. A local guide is worth considering for an off-season climb or if you are climbing alone at night.
