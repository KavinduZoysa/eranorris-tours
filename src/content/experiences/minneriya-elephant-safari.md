## What the Gathering is

Minneriya and Kaudulla are national parks built around ancient irrigation reservoirs, known in Sri Lanka as tanks. Minneriya's was built by King Mahasen in the 3rd century AD. In the rainy months the water spreads across the tank beds. As the dry season sets in, it slowly drops back, and fresh grass grows on the exposed ground. Elephants come out of the surrounding forest to graze it, drink and bathe, often in family herds with young calves.

At its height, usually around August and September, more than 400 elephants have been counted using Minneriya's grasslands. It is one of the largest seasonal gatherings of Asian elephants anywhere, and in Sri Lanka it is known simply as "the Gathering".

It depends on the water. From about 2019, water released into the tanks during the dry season kept the beds flooded, and the Gathering almost disappeared: in September 2021 only about twenty elephants were counted. After changes in water management it was back in strength in 2024 and 2025. In late 2025 the irrigation authorities gave a court undertaking to keep the natural rise and fall of the water.

## Minneriya, Kaudulla or Hurulu

The same elephants use three neighbouring areas:

- **Minneriya National Park.** Around 8,900 ha surrounding the Minneriya tank, between Habarana and [Polonnaruwa](/places/polonnaruwa/). It is the best-known park for the Gathering.
- **Kaudulla National Park.** To the north, linked to Minneriya by a protected forest corridor. Herds often move here later in the dry season, from around September.
- **Hurulu Eco Park.** Part of the Hurulu Forest Reserve near Habarana, a UNESCO biosphere reserve since 1977. It is run by the Forest Department rather than the wildlife department. Drivers often use it when the herds are not in the other two parks, and in 2025 it drew more foreign visitors than Minneriya and Kaudulla together.

Because the herds move, safari drivers in Habarana and [Sigiriya](/places/sigiriya/) usually choose the park on the day, from where elephants have been seen recently. The best park is whichever one the herds are using that week.

## When to go

- **About July to October** is the usual season for the Gathering at Minneriya, with the peak around August and September. In some years it begins earlier, as the water starts to fall from about May.
- **From about late October** the north-east monsoon brings rain, the tanks fill and the herds spread out again. Late in the year, Kaudulla or Hurulu is often the better choice.
- **Outside the season**, safaris still run, but the herds are smaller, more scattered and harder to predict.

Most safaris run in the afternoon, from about 3 pm until the park closes, because the herds tend to shelter in the forest through the heat and come out onto the grassland late in the day. Morning drives are quieter, but the elephants may be harder to find in the open.

Nothing is guaranteed: rain, water levels and the herds' movements change from week to week.

## What a safari involves

Visits are by open safari jeep with a driver. Jeeps are hired mainly in Habarana and Sigiriya. On top of the jeep hire, there is a park entrance charge for each person and vehicle. You pay it at the park ticket office, or book it in advance through the wildlife department's online permit system where the park is listed. Make sure you receive a valid ticket or permit. In late 2025 the department took action against jeeps that had taken visitors into Kaudulla without tickets.

The department has said it is planning a single ticketing system for Minneriya and Kaudulla, so the arrangements may change.

A typical drive lasts about three hours on rough, dusty tracks, mostly around the grassland at the edge of the tank. As well as elephants, you are likely to see spotted deer, toque macaques and water birds such as painted storks, pelicans and cormorants. Leopards and sloth bears live in the parks but are rarely seen.

Bring a hat, sunscreen, water, a scarf against the dust, and binoculars or a zoom lens.

## Watching responsibly

In the peak weeks, 400 to 600 jeeps a day have been reported entering Minneriya and Kaudulla. They tend to converge on the same herds, and families with young calves are easily stressed. Sri Lanka Tourism's guideline for safari operators says jeeps must keep to the marked tracks, stay at or below 30 km/h and give animals the right of way. What you ask of your driver also makes a difference:

- Ask the driver to keep a good distance, switch off the engine when you stop, and never surround a herd or block its way to the water or the forest.
- Give mothers with calves and lone bulls extra room. Move away if an elephant flaps its ears, shakes its head or walks towards the jeep.
- Stay in the jeep, keep your voice down and avoid sudden movements.
- Never feed elephants, in the park or on the roads around it. It is prohibited, and it teaches them to approach vehicles.
- If too many jeeps gather around one herd, ask the driver to move on. A quieter sighting is usually a better one.

Wild elephants also cross the main roads around Habarana and Minneriya, especially around dusk. Vehicles should slow down and give them room, and nobody should get out for a photo.

The elephant rides advertised around Habarana use captive, working elephants. They are not a wildlife safari and not a way of seeing elephants in the wild.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better, Minneriya or Kaudulla?

Neither on its own. The same elephants move between the two parks, so the better one is wherever the herds are that week, and drivers usually decide on the day. Minneriya is best known for the peak of the Gathering; Kaudulla is often quieter.

### When is the best time to see the elephant Gathering?

Usually August and September, within a wider season of about July to October. Numbers rise and fall with the water level and change from year to year, so no date is certain.

### Can I do the safari from Polonnaruwa or Kandy?

From Polonnaruwa, yes. Minneriya's entrance is about 25–30 km away, so many people see the ruins in the morning and take a safari in the afternoon. Kandy is around 2.5–3 hours each way, so stay nearer the parks.

### Are elephant sightings guaranteed?

No. In the dry season sightings are likely, but numbers vary from day to day and year to year. Leave a spare afternoon if it matters to you.
